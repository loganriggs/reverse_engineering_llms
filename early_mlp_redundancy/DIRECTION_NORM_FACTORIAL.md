# EMLP-P9: magnitude explains the large agreement transfer advantage

2026-10-28. Completes P8's fixed factorial on the same five competent cohorts, without new
prompts, fitted gains or further competence filtering. P8 supplied three combinations of
old/new response direction and old/new norm. P9 adds the fourth: new direction at old norm.

## Results

Percent reduction in prefixed donor-output KL from the intact prefixed recipient:

| Cohort | Old direction, old norm | New direction, old norm | Old direction, new norm | New direction, new norm |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Agreement A |46.36|48.57|16.43|18.62|
| Agreement B |34.23|39.83|16.68|19.67|
| Plural noun A |17.79|17.40|13.15|12.95|
| Plural noun B |7.18|9.51|7.22|9.38|
| Sentiment B |15.46|17.14|16.98|18.72|

A is `Consider the following text. `; B is `Read this carefully: `. Norms are per example,
not cohort-average fitted gains. The old norm is not universally larger or better.

For agreement, switching to the old norm raises recovery by roughly 18–30 percentage points,
whereas changing direction at fixed norm changes recovery by roughly 2–6 points. The old-vector
advantage in P8 therefore does not establish that its direction is more useful. At fixed norm,
the new direction performs better in both agreement cohorts. Sentiment and pluralization have
smaller, different patterns; do not generalize the agreement magnitude result to every task.

These interventions suggest a broadly transferable direction with meaningful context-dependent
gain and a smaller contextual direction change, for the tested agreement pairs. They do not
show that natural gain is optimal, that the model implements a scalar gate, or that donor recovery
measures fidelity to the natural computation. A stronger artificial edit can move closer to the
donor while being less faithful to the actual intermediate response. No confidence or population
claim is made from these small, reused training panels.

## Next: test sharing across word pairs

Stop adding preambles. The next fixed diagnostic uses agreement and sentiment, where the
paired edits share an interpretable relation: singular→plural or positive→negative, respectively.
The plural-noun panel instead changes cat→dog, book→car, etc.; its edit direction is not a common
pluralization operation. A family-average vector there would confound unrelated lexical changes.

Use four leave-one-lexical-pair-out folds per family. Exclude both directions, all suffixes and
all preambles of the test lexical pair from constructing the direction. Use only original-context
training responses from the other three lexical pairs. Deduplicate exact source prefixes (the
agreement suffix variants do not generate independent responses), average unit response vectors
within each training lexical pair, then average equally across the three pairs and normalize.
Estimate forward and reverse directions separately; do not assume antisymmetry.

Apply that direction to the held-out pair in P8's passing prefixed cohorts. First match the
held-out native response's norm explicitly as an ORACLE magnitude, to isolate directional reuse.
Compare native response, same-lexical original direction at native norm, cross-lexical mean
direction at native norm, and four norm-matched signed-permutation controls. Report vector
alignment and donor KL/task-margin recovery per lexical fold, plus pooled cohort summaries.
No inference-time output labels or gain sweep. Native response magnitude is unavailable to a
standalone predictor, so success would motivate predicting magnitude, not prove a full featurizer.
Failure would reject one shared mean direction, not all shared low-dimensional subspaces.

This is cross-validation on previously inspected training data: held out from this direction
estimate, not an untouched scientific holdout. Preserve the original whole-task evaluation
exclusions. Do not tune folds or include failed P8 cohorts. The outcome should decide whether to
compare a shared subspace with context-dependent predictors or first broaden causal targets.
