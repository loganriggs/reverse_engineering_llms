# Joint source selection repairs the four-source inner-bracket failure

The [source-sufficiency sweep](NESTED_SOURCE_SUFFICIENCY_RESULTS.md) found that retaining each head's four largest absolute QK scores preserves outer-bracket closing but destroys much of the **inner** opener-edit response. This experiment tests whether four positions per head are inherently too few, or whether independent source ranking misses a useful combination. The one-block model and exact QK×OV source replay are unchanged.

`eval_nested_joint_source_search.py` begins with the score-magnitude top-four mask. It repeatedly proposes replacing one selected source with any other past position in the **same head**, accepting the replacement that best matches the full teacher probability distribution. In the primary arm, the objective sees only the original prefix. The selected mask is then frozen while testing a valid inner/outer opener edit and a distinct no-effect control edit. It uses no closer labels, oracle probabilities, or edited activations to choose the mask. A second arm also sees the relevant edited teacher output; that arm is an in-sample feasibility check, not a held-out causal test. Every step preserves exactly four positions per head and can keep the incumbent choice, so the original-prefix objective cannot increase. This is local per-prefix search, not sparse model training or a fixed canonical basis.

| Independent panel | Selection | Inner opener-edit L1 | Inner edited accuracy | Outer opener-edit L1 | Verb oracle KL |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Seed 20261208, 117 inner / 240 outer, 2 swap sweeps | Score top-four | 2.723 | 76.9% | .0060 | .3383 |
| Same | Original-only swaps | **.0883** | **100%** | **.0027** | **.2891** |
| Seed 20261209, 124 inner / 264 outer, 4 swap sweeps | Score top-four | 2.642 | 82.3% | .0082 | .3122 |
| Same | Original-only swaps | **.0915** | **100%** | **.0019** | **.2696** |

The native verb oracle KL is .2662 and .2588 on those two verb panels. Thus four jointly selected sources per head largely recover the inner edit and improve verb prediction, but **do not yet match native verb KL or the earlier K=8 score-magnitude reduction**. Mean teacher KL across original, relevant edit, and control at inner sites falls from 2.595 to .0301 on seed08 and from 2.478 to .0319 on seed09. Crucially, the edited teacher KL (.0303/.0319) stays close to original-prefix KL (.0298/.0300), even though the edit was unseen by the original-only search. No-effect control probability-change L1 after search is .0002/.0004. The native-logit source replay discrepancy remains below `1e-5`.

Seeing the relevant edit during mask search gives little additional benefit: paired-swap inner edit L1 is .1038 on seed08 and .0890 on seed09. Those numbers cannot be read as out-of-sample performance; the original-only arm is the more meaningful causal transfer result. Both arms are coordinate-descent heuristics, so failure of either would not prove that no four-source subset exists.

The optimized mask is not merely selecting the relevant opener more often: on seed09, its inner-opener inclusion rises only from .315 to .337 of heads. The selected source pattern changes more broadly. At inner sites, score ranking chooses lag 9 (the delimiter immediately before the inner opener) 335 times across heads; original-only search keeps it 84 times and instead increases lag 6 from 144 to 231. These are token-position associations, not yet a semantic explanation of the head computations. They show why the large K=4 failure should be attributed to the **ranking rule**, rather than taken as evidence for an irreducible eight-source budget or for cancellation without further intervention tests.

Next, test whether a fixed, weight-only token-pair basis or a small learned gate transfers these combinations across prefixes and held-out task families. A per-instance mask with access to the teacher output is an interpretability diagnostic, not a deployable circuit description.
