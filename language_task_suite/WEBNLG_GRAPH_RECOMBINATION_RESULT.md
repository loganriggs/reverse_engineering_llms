# Broad WebNLG graph recombination fails the semantic and form gates

This pilot tests whether the enriched WebNLG role annotations can yield **new fact combinations** with varied human-attested phrasing. The [candidate generator](webnlg_graph_recombination.py) groups `good` training lexicalizations by category and role-labeled predicate graph. It selects a form from one entry, entity values from another with the same graph shape, and sometimes replaces one patient/bridge role with a third entry's value. The resulting triples are constructed from the actual chosen roles. The auditor checks mapped role coverage, rendered-slot coverage, and graph novelty. These structural checks are deliberately weaker than English truth-conditional validation.

Across 512 sampled candidates, the bank exposed 937 reusable graph shapes and 11,387 forms. There were 227 role crosses and 124 normalized triple graphs absent from the source bank. Only 235 generated texts reached 20 words for the equal-length form screen. On these crops, cross-document repeated-trigram share was **.470**, versus **.464** on the associated original human WebNLG source forms and **.008** on a size-matched random FineWeb development panel. The source-human and generated eligible counts differ slightly, so the close .470/.464 comparison is descriptive. The gap to FineWeb is too large to treat this as a more realistic language DGP.

Manual inspection of 16 generated candidates found several independent failure modes despite complete role maps: a swapped bridge value could put Dublin in Japan, a template could call a national title the capital of a country, an unbound source-specific leader name could remain in a supposedly delexicalized form, and some sentences were ungrammatical even without role crossing. Some examples were coherent, but there is no validated accept rate. The structural checks do not catch false predicates, hidden source claims, or category/type mismatches; the corpus `good` label also does not guarantee transfer to a new graph.

**Decision:** Do not train interpretability models on broad WebNLG role recombination or promote it as the target DGP. Retain it as a negative control for the distinction between role alignment and semantic validation. A small manually reviewed relation subset could still be useful for exact synthetic causal tests, but it is unlikely to close the natural-form gap on its own. Redirect the main realistic-language effort toward **naturally occurring rule-bearing documents** with high-precision visible-text labels and sampled document/genre/task rates, then add only counterfactual edits that pass independent semantic and fluency checks. This empirical carrier branch will not have a full symbolic next-token oracle, so report rule-site ground truth and ordinary-token uncertainty separately. Keep the explicit semantic-graph branch as a controlled laboratory for intervention/circuit checks, and compare the two rather than claiming either solves both realism and exact semantics.
