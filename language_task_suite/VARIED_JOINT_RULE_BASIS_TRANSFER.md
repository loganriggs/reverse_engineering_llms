# Which compact directions preserve the varied language's rule effects?

The [varied joint-rule model](SURFACE_MIXTURE_V2_VARIED_RESULTS.md) is competent on held-out next-token probabilities and responds directionally to lexical, induction, previous-place and cue edits. This experiment asks a narrower interpretability question: at a fixed attention output bond, can a compact direction set preserve each **model causal effect** on new sentence frames? A featurizer fitted on all four known rule families is compared with one fitted without the tested family. Neither is task discovery: the language model was trained on every rule and frame, and the analyst chose the intervention families.

`eval_surface_v2_rule_mediation.py` now accepts the `v2_varied` checkpoint, including the new cue and nuisance-determiner edits. The fit frames are `lead/at/when`, and test frames are `subject/in_place/during`; the reverse split swaps them. Both splits contain a cue-bearing frame, unlike the old three-plus-three split. Fit and test use different generator seeds, 256 streams of 72 tokens each. The model is the same width-64, two-layer, four-head bilinear checkpoint; all candidate bases have the same width within each comparison. An additional test changes only the evaluation parenthetical rate from 0.2 to 0.1.

The measurement is a **last-position local patch** immediately before the second attention output projection. The complete edited-minus-original vector is patched first. Its effect differs from the full edited-model probability effect by mean L1 **0.066/0.058** for induction and **0.029/0.031** for previous-place (forward/reverse). The site is therefore useful for those two rules. For lexical edits the difference is **1.145/1.194**, and for cues **0.435/0.407**; those rules have important paths outside this one local vector. A compact basis's local success on lexical or cue edits cannot be presented as recovery of the full computation.

At width eight, the table gives mean L1 error between each restricted patch's probability shift and the complete *local* patch's shift. Lower is better. The supervised `own edit` basis is fitted on normalized response vectors from the same intervention family but disjoint frames; `pooled` balances all four relevant families; `leave out` omits the tested family. `output verbs` is derived from the model's output weights using the **known set of 12 verb tokens**, with no sampled activations. It is output-target-aware, not a task-blind discovery method.

The ranking largely persists when only the test generator uses parenthetical rate 0.1: own-edit/pooled/leave-out errors for induction are **.141/.241/.304** forward and **.142/.283/.303** reverse; for previous place, **.142/.197/.331** forward and **.160/.265/.466** reverse. These are changes in the data distribution of sentence form, not new model training. The learned bases were never fitted on the lower-rate test pairs.

The output-weight comparator sharpens the meaning of “canonicalization.” The 12 centered verb-output rows have **rank 11** after composition with the attention output weight. Their 11 right singular directions preserve the complete local patch within L1 **.043/.046** for induction and **.031/.032** for previous place. At width 16, the first 11 output-contrast directions are completed with explicitly orthogonalized, ranked attention-weight directions; errors fall to **.007/.008** and **.006/.006**. This is a strong compression of the local *verb-output function*, but much of its advantage follows from knowing the evaluated output set. It is not a decomposition into induction, lexical, place and cue computations. The first 11 dimensions have no rule labels, and preserving a full local patch does not repair the model's remaining error against the exact oracle.

An earlier-bond control reinforces the site distinction. At the first attention bond, a complete last-position patch carries only about .02 total-variation induction effect and .01–.02 previous-place effect, compared with native effects around .21–.22. Lexical and cue effects are partial there too. Comparing compact bases against that first-bond full patch would mostly measure geometry of a weak route.

The causal supervision is informative: own-rule bases beat generic energy bases for induction and previous-place effects at width eight, while pooling several rules trades away some family-specific fidelity. Excluding previous-place edits hurts strongly in both frame directions, so the pooled representation does not discover that rule from the others. A quartimax rotation of a 32-dimensional weight prior followed by axis selection does **not** consistently beat the own-edit SVD; see saved JSON for all sparse-rotation, nuisance and oracle-effect metrics. Nuisance adjective and determiner edits have near-zero oracle effects and small model effects, so their low patch errors are a selectivity check rather than a difficult compression result.

This is one model seed and one bond-level probe. It distinguishes **response-space reduction** from **rule identification**. The next decisive step is to train a shared-dictionary or other structured featurizer with an explicit causal objective, while maintaining a whole-rule-family featurizer holdout and a scalar Top-K control. It should earn a rule-discovery claim only if unlabeled groups predict effects on held-out combinations or a new rule family beyond what this output-contrast basis and the supervised own-edit basis already explain. Also retain ordinary reconstruction, native-model effect fidelity, and exact-oracle effect fidelity as separate measurements.

Reproduce with `eval_surface_v2_rule_mediation.py --model-dir language_task_suite/surface_mixture_v2_varied_soft_models --layer 1 --nfit 256 --ntest 256`, `--width 8`, `11`, or `16`, with and without `--reverse-frames`; add `--test-parenthetical-mass 0.1` for the form-rate shift. Results are saved alongside the checkpoint as `rule_mediation_*_n256.json`, `rule_mediation_*_w11.json`, `rule_mediation_*_w16_defined.json`, and `rule_mediation_*_low_bracket.json`.
