# Output-aware shared groups on the varied joint-rule model

The [varied two-layer bilinear language model](SURFACE_MIXTURE_V2_VARIED_RESULTS.md) has a causally relevant final attention bond for induction and previous-place verb effects. The [basis comparison](VARIED_JOINT_RULE_BASIS_TRANSFER.md) found that eight intervention-fitted directions preserve these effects better than generic energy bases, while output-weight contrasts compress the local verb response without identifying its cause. This experiment asks whether the proposed shared-dictionary/group-gate architecture improves when trained with a known verb-output contrast objective. The answer is **no at this site and budget**: a scalar Top-8 dictionary remains better on the omitted previous-place family.

`train_surface_v2_shared_groups.py` now accepts the varied generator and a paired, locally linearized output-contrast loss. For a supported lexical or induction edit, the model supplies pre-output-projection bond activations $z_0,z_1$. The featurizer reconstructs both endpoints separately. In addition to activation reconstruction, the new objective penalizes the difference between reconstructed and native changes after multiplication by the centered 12-verb output-weight map composed with this bond's output weight. The map has unit mean row norm so the coefficient is comparable with a unit-variance activation-change MSE. This gives the featurizer the **known verb output set and paired edit contexts**; it is supervised and is not actual downstream KL or a task-blind discovery objective.

The fit panel balances lexical and induction edits; validation uses a different stream seed. The entire previous-place **edit family** is excluded from fit/validation pairs and evaluated on the opposite sentence-frame set and a third seed. Forward/reverse splits use `lead/at/when` versus `subject/in_place/during`. The language model itself was trained on all rules and frames. A previous-place proposal can still be present in a lexical or induction context, so the holdout concerns the *intervention family*, not all information about place. Each fit uses 1,200 optimizer steps; the paired-objective methods share minibatch indices and initial PCA directions. All grouped designs can activate at most eight atoms. `16×4, 2 gates` has 16 groups of four atoms and two active groups; `32×2, 4 gates` tests a less rigid grouping with 32 groups of two atoms and four active groups. The disjoint group control and scalar Top-6/Top-8 comparators are trained under the same objective within each run.

The table reports mean L1 error of the restricted probability effect relative to the **complete local bond patch** on held-out previous-place edits. Lower is better. The complete local patch differs from the full model effect by only about .03 L1 on these panels, so this is a meaningful causal site. Forward/reverse use separate frame directions.

| Objective and group layout | Shared groups F/R | Fixed disjoint F/R | Scalar Top-6 F/R | Scalar Top-8 F/R |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Reconstruction, 16×4 | .443/.330 | .446/.373 | .196/.198 | **.168/.170** |
| Reconstruction + output contrast, 16×4 | .465/.416 | .438/.344 | .199/.200 | **.203/.184** |
| Reconstruction + output contrast, 32×2 | .373/.282 | .396/.362 | .199/.200 | **.203/.184** |

The 32×2 layout improves the shared-group result over 16×4 but remains worse than scalar Top-8 on both splits. For held-out induction edits, its patch-to-full error is **.290/.281** versus scalar Top-8 **.181/.182**. The 32×2 model uses **7.09/7.27** active atoms on average and covers **41/45 of 64** dictionary atoms; the scalar control uses eight. The comparison does not prove group sharing is intrinsically worse: the active-support combinatorics and optimization differ, and the output contrast is only a linearized proxy. It does show that this variant does not meet the current causal-fidelity bar despite more flexible grouping and output-aware supervision. At 16×4, adding the output objective worsens the scalar and shared-group previous-place scores relative to reconstruction-only, so there is no evidence that this objective repairs the earlier negative result.

This is still far short of the user's earlier **iterated L1 → prune → revert → finetune** interpretability result. In [the original basis-aligned block experiment](/home/loganriggs/Coding/reverse_engineer/tensor_language/basis_aligned/RESULTS.md), folding weights into the input/output basis exposed the invariant interaction form. Sparsifying only middle factors produced cosmetically sparse matrices with most off-distribution cross-block junk intact; sparsifying all factors from a rotated known solution recovered the planted block structure with zero cross-junk and near-exact off-distribution behavior. The current featurizer work offers local prediction-effect compression, not comparably clear QK/OV/direct computation paths. The original result also warns that low parameter count or apparent coordinate sparsity alone is insufficient.

Stop sweeping this reconstruction-plus-linear-output objective on the same bond unless a structural change beats scalar Top-8 at matched active support. The next direct benchmark should take the **current trained bilinear language model**, run the original iterative sparsification protocol with a fixed held-out exact-oracle KL and causal-edit panel, and compare attention-only versus embedding/output-inclusive pruning. Then fold the surviving first-layer QK and OV paths into token/output coordinates, test exact replay and causal removal, and ask whether the sparse paths name the known lexical, induction, previous-place and cue proposals. Preserving model behavior is the entry gate; path-level transfer and selectivity are the interpretation bar.

Reproduce the paired experiment with `train_surface_v2_shared_groups.py --model-dir language_task_suite/surface_mixture_v2_varied_soft_models --nfit 1024 --nval 256 --ntest 256 --steps 1200 --coverage-weight 1`, adding `--output-contrast-weight 1` for output-aware fitting and `--groups 32 --group-budget 4 --membership-budget 2` for the wider group layout. Add `--reverse-frames` for the other split. The six result directories named `surface_mixture_v2_varied_groups{_recon,_output1,32_output1}_{forward,reverse}` contain histories, group diagnostics, model/featurizer weights, reconstruction, change and causal-patch metrics.
