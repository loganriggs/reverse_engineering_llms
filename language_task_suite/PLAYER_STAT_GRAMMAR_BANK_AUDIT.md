# Whole-clause player-stat grammar: a safer natural-form lower bound

The [box-score inventory](BOXSCORE_RELATION_INVENTORY.md) found thousands of matching numeric statements, but many carry unsupported ranking, history, game-flow, injury, or qualitative claims. The original [strict clause screen](GROUNDED_PLAYER_SENTENCE_BANK_YIELD.md) allowed only 13 RotoWire and 33 SBNation training clauses, partly because it rejected all spelled numbers and many ordinary tally verbs. This audit tests a narrow, complete-clause grammar that permits those common forms while verifying every stated quantity against the structured box score. It is a **candidate bank lower bound**, not an oracle for arbitrary human report prose.

The [screen](audit_player_stat_grammar_bank.py) requires a single full player name at sentence start; exactly one complete declarative tally clause; a limited set of verbs such as *had*, *posted*, *tallied*, *finished with*, and *scored*; and a conjunction of explicit PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, TO, or FG3M quantities. Digits and written numbers zero–twenty are recognized. Every quantity must equal that player's box score, every numeric token must belong to a recognized quantity, and no field may repeat. Another roster player's surname disqualifies a clause. The whole-sentence grammar excludes trailing rank, outcome, bench, historical, game-flow, or evaluative claims. It intentionally rejects many valid natural sentences; it does not infer their semantics from a mere bag of matching numbers.

Manual review of the printed, uncommitted examples found plain tally statements, including a player finishing with points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks, and another adding assists and steals. Adversarial fixtures confirmed that a mismatched rebound count, an extra year, another named player, and an unsupported win suffix are rejected. Aggregate counts, hashed top forms, and no source prose are saved in the [result JSON](results/player_stat_grammar_bank.json). The held-out group is by game key, not by clause row.

This improves the **semantically constrained** RotoWire train yield from 13 to 153 and shows a real benefit from spelling and extended box-score fields. It also resolves the earlier apparent gap: 3,069 numerically matching RotoWire clauses are *not* a safe sentence bank, while 13 was a deliberately harsh whitelist result. The new grammar still supplies fewer than one accepted clause for most games and only 20 train games with two. Sampling multiple independent clauses from it could also make discourse awkward (for example, repeated *also*). It cannot yet carry a realistic variable document DGP. Before training a new model on it, extend the sampled world and semantic validator to support checkable relations such as player-to-team outcome, starter status, and score ranking; audit those relations on fresh game-disjoint data and compare both full-document and task-window form with natural reports. Do not relax the grammar by accepting a matching number while leaving an unmodeled claim intact.
