# Partial initial facts: correct parser, repetitive documents

The objective is a varied, language-like **sampled** distribution with known rules for comparing weight-based interpretation across DGP settings. V11 and V12 test whether breaking the fixed three-object introduction block into independently sampled facts helps. These are generator experiments; no model capability or weight claim follows from them.

V11 samples the order and one of three forms for each object's owner, color, place, and label. Its visible-prefix checker accumulates those facts, constructs an ordinary canonical introduction after the four facts for an object are present, and then uses the independently checked V8–V10 event parser. The checker reads no latent state or sampled lexical map. Two 300-document panels, with and without typed lexical variation, pass all **7,200** initial claims, **2,968** answer sites, and matched upstream edits. Both panels change every document relative to the complete-introduction baseline. [V11 semantic audit](results/v11_initial_facts_semantic_audit_300x2.json).

But V11 turns three introductory sentences into twelve short inventory statements. On four paired 64×100-word panels, its first-40 repeated-word fraction is **.432–.450**, versus **.245–.274** for V8; top-ten word share is **.464–.490**, versus **.312–.337**. Typed lexical variation does not repair those structural frequencies. The first scored answer also moves later. [Reference-prevalence panel](results/v11_initial_facts_paired_reference_n64x2.json); [task-rich panel](results/v11_initial_facts_paired_source50_n64x2.json).

V12 tests a less exhaustive version. It makes an initial fact mandatory only when an event or scored query needs it, then includes other facts with probability .35. Every queried alias has all candidate object labels visible, so an alias swap remains a supported causal edit. The generator disables V8 incidental/context clauses because they could otherwise reveal facts marked as omitted. The visible checker fills unobserved state slots with inert placeholders solely to reuse the old event parser; those placeholders are never scored as answers. The checker itself does not read the latent selection plan.

Two further 300-document panels pass **2,875** answer sites and matched upstream edits. They also pass **2,365 omission-neutrality interventions**: changing an omitted initial fact leaves the entire visible document and answer sequence unchanged. About eight of twelve initial facts are visible per document in this audit. [V12 semantic audit](results/v12_partial_facts_semantic_audit_300x2.json).

V12 improves on V11 but remains substantially less language-like than the V8 baseline in its own paired screen, where incidental/context clauses are disabled for every arm. At reference prevalence, partial-fact plus typed-lexical documents have top-ten share **.415/.419** and first-40 repetition **.424/.423**; the paired V8 baseline has **.333/.341** and **.244/.278**. At task-rich prevalence, partial-fact plus lexical gives **.432/.429** and **.445/.434**; V8 gives **.324/.316** and **.264/.245**. The saved independent FineWeb panels are much farther still: top-ten share .207/.217 and first-40 repetition .160/.189. [Reference-prevalence panel](results/v12_partial_facts_paired_reference_n64x2.json); [task-rich panel](results/v12_partial_facts_paired_source50_n64x2.json).

An actual V12 opening reads: “helmet carried the label River. helmet was indigo. monitor belonged to Wendy. monitor was in the arena. Pine was the recorded label for monitor.” Every claim is true, but the text is plainly a shuffled inventory. The failure is not a parser bug or lack of lexical breadth; it is the document-level composition rule. Additional short fact templates repeat *was*, *the*, *recorded*, *label*, and object names more often than natural prose.

To check whether V12 merely chose the wrong fact budget, I swept the optional-fact rate from zero to one on two additional paired 128-document panels, measuring the first 40 words of **every** document. Mean visible facts rose from about six to twelve, yet repeated-word fraction stayed .431–.443 at every rate; the paired complete-introduction baseline was .252/.262. Top-ten share stayed .525–.542 versus .351 for the baseline in this 40-word protocol. This closes the easy “mention fewer initial facts” adjustment. [Fact-budget sweep](results/v12_optional_fact_rate_first40_n128x2.json). These 40-word top-ten values should not be compared directly with the earlier 100-word FineWeb panels.

The partial-state parser and omission-neutrality audit are reusable, but neither V11 nor V12 should be a new training corpus merely for apparent variability. The next DGP design should start from sampled **document purposes and propositions**, decide which facts are worth mentioning, and realize them with variable clause packing, coordination, reference, and genre conventions. Compare both full-document and early-window form on independent natural-text panels, and keep exact supported-edit checks. Once the distribution improves materially, train the model grid, gate each learned rule behaviorally, and compare weight-only and data-aware canonicalization/sparsification on those eligible rules.
