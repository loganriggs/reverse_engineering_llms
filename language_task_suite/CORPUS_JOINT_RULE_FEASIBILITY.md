# Joint rules in heterogeneous natural web prose

The goal is a variable sampled language distribution with known rule sites for testing weight-based interpretability. This pass asks whether two existing controlled transformations can coexist inside one FineWeb document while preserving its prose. The unmodified web document supplies surface form; it is not a known generative grammar and has no full next-token oracle.

## Spelling convention × bracket closure

The [joint generator](corpus_joint_style_delimiter_dgp.py) selects two naturally occurring sites in the same document: a US/UK spelling cue and later word from a different lexical family, and a prose parenthetical. It constructs four arms, US/UK × round/square. The spelling transformation rewrites all supported lowercase variant words consistently; the bracket transformation changes exactly the selected opener and closer. An independent audit checks the visible cue and answer, opposite answers under each factor, exact bracket-edit locality, and that both answer sites and their sources fit inside a 320-token GPT-2 BPE window.

On 1,000 FineWeb `sample-10BT` documents at offsets 83,500–84,499, cropped to 8,000 characters, 97 had a spelling-rule opportunity, 350 had a parenthetical, 36 had a pair within 1,200 characters, and **18** yielded a complete aligned four-arm factorial. The aligned source-to-target spelling gaps range from 20 to 165 words. On these 18 selected documents, the original and edited first-100-word panels have the same 20.94% top-ten word share and 0.79% repeated-across-document trigram share. This near-identity reflects localized edits to human prose; it does not establish general-population realism for a selected 1.8% subset. See the [reproducible audit](audit_corpus_joint_style_delimiter.py) and [aggregate JSON](results/corpus_joint_style_delimiter_audit_skip83500_n1000.json).

This pairing is valid as a narrow two-rule fixture but sparse as the main training distribution. More importantly, the prior single-rule spelling pilot failed held-out cue-response checks even with 1,970 training windows and paired augmentation. Training only on the 1.8% joint subset would reduce exposure further. Do not claim a learned joint spelling circuit from this audit. A mixture in which rules occur independently, with whole-combination holdouts, is more informative than conditioning *all* training documents on both sites.

## Literal uppercase recurrence × bracket closure

A separate [opportunity screen](audit_corpus_uppercase_repeat_joint.py) tests three- to five-letter uppercase strings that recur 20–300 words apart. On the next 1,000 FineWeb documents (offsets 84,500–85,499), 234 had such recurrence, 353 had a prose parenthetical, 102 had both somewhere, 81 had both within 1,200 characters, and **58** had a complete source/target span within 320 BPE tokens. Insisting that the repeated uppercase answer occupy one token would retain only 27 of those documents; multiple-token span supervision materially improves usable yield. The [aggregate result](results/corpus_uppercase_repeat_joint_skip84500_n1000.json) has no source text.

Manual inspection of 12 deterministic joint candidates found very different kinds of uppercase strings: agencies and brands, a disease name, an era marker, a country label, and product formats. The surface-repeat rule is often visible, but these are not one semantic type. A generic acronym-to-acronym substitution could make the surrounding prose false or nonsensical, and some repeats may not be one referent. This is an **opportunity screen**, not a validated generator or behavioral result. The next implementation needs typed replacement or an explicitly literal-copy interpretation, span-level token supervision, and a manual naturalness/identity red team before model training.

These results argue for preserving the broad natural-document mixture and sampling rule prevalence independently. Requiring every document to contain every task creates a narrow, selected genre distribution. Method comparisons should still score learned rules separately on held-out documents and supported edits, while reporting reconstruction, prediction, causal specificity, and new-rule discovery as distinct outcomes.
