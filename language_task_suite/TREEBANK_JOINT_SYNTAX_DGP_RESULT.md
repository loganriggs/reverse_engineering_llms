# Joint corpus-derived syntax rules: correct labels, unresolved realism

This experiment tests whether a corpus-derived syntax backbone can put several known rules in the same sampled document without the extreme sentence-frame repetition of the symbolic world generator. It is a multi-rule **syntax** candidate, not a semantic document/world model. The official public treebank from [Jumelet and Zuidema's PCFG resources](https://github.com/clclab/pcfg-lm) was restored locally at the previously audited SHA-256 `6e8c4a8910d7da3145cacd93589f12f21cfb059b3073019c127ea39dbb451087`; source sentences are not committed.

The [sampler](treebank_joint_syntax_document.py) draws four clauses per document. Each clause has a corpus-derived noun phrase, a sampled finite `be`/`have`/`do` lemma and corpus-derived VP continuation, and the number-appropriate inflection. It may insert a short corpus PP inside parentheses before the verb. Later clauses may repeat a previous subject phrase. The three scored families are agreement inflection, closing parenthesis, and exact subject-phrase recurrence. The verb lemma remains sampled/latent until its inflection; a visible subject fixes number, not the full-vocabulary next token. Independent site checks inspect the visible subject, bracket stack, or earlier phrase. A second sampling policy preserves complete clauses from the source treebank; a third swaps a noun phrase only with another phrase of the same surface head and number.

On 512 documents from a 100k-tree fit, all 2,048 agreement sites, 512–533 closing-parenthesis sites, and 529–552 subject-reuse sites in the three joint arms passed the visible checks. All 3,101–3,129 deliberately wrong site tokens were rejected in each arm. The free-recombination arm had 278/512 documents with all three families; source-complete and same-head arms had 253/512 and 258/512. This establishes co-occurrence and rule-label integrity, **not** natural meaning or model competence. For full rule transfer, future training would hold out whole phrases and whole family combinations.

The form tradeoff is sharp. On first-60-word samples, the independently sampled, *without-replacement* disjoint treebank reference had 10.4% cross-document repeated-trigram occurrence share and 23.3% top-ten word share. Free recombination had 19.0% and 29.2%; source-complete clauses had 66.1% and 31.6%; same-head substitution reduced those to 56.4% and 30.2%. Agreement-site windows showed 10.6%, 53.1%, and 42.4% repeated-trigram shares respectively. The reference is four independently chosen treebank sentences, not a coherent natural document or FineWeb-matched task sample. Generated documents also vary in mean length and retain some treebank tokenization artifacts, so these are diagnostics rather than a complete realism score.

Manual inspection supplies the decisive limit: free recombination can pair a noun phrase with a predicate that is syntactically legal but semantically nonsensical. Source-complete clauses are more plausible locally but the 100k-tree fit yields only 684 usable distinct clauses, so repeated material dominates when sampling thousands of sentences. Same-head substitution adds 1,331 phrase changes in 512 documents, but keeps most continuation text fixed and does not close the repetition gap.

I tested a [GPT-2 form prior](audit_treebank_lm_form_prior.py) on a separate 512-document sample: 271 contained all three rules, and the lowest-loss 100 were compared with 100 random eligible documents. Mean GPT-2 subword NLL improved from 4.60 to 4.16, but the selected documents still had implausible proposition combinations. Their mean length rose from 45.5 to 49.8 words, top-ten word share from 30.0% to 31.1%, and repeated-trigram share from 6.5% to 7.8% (on the smaller 100-document comparison). GPT-2 likelihood is therefore not an adequate semantic-compatibility prior by itself. It may reward fluent local phrasing and recurring subjects while missing whether the proposition makes sense.

**Decision:** Retain the joint sampler as a correctness fixture and as a negative control for form priors. Do not train a bilinear model on it as the main improved-language DGP or use it to claim natural-language rule circuits. The next generator needs a typed semantic event or proposition first, followed by a corpus-calibrated realization conditioned on that meaning. Accept a realization only if a separate visible-text checker recovers the intended relation and supported edits stay consistent. The small treebank agreement and bracket components can be reused there, but corpus-subtree recombination alone does not supply semantic compatibility.
