# Section-scoped facts in the joint toy language

The [disjoint FineWeb review](FINEWEB_DISJOINT_PANEL_CHECK.md) found clear local scope in five of five cue-selected numbered-item windows, while broad attribution and arithmetic cues were much less reliable. This motivated a controlled, exact-oracle section task alongside the existing v6 verb/object mixtures, object-place questions, and source-attributed claims. It is a **test of one natural-language-like dependency**, not a model of FineWeb or a complete implementation of the original mixed n-gram/induction/bracket proposal.

`surface_mixture_v7.py` samples numbered sections with distinct badge colors. A heading opens a scope; a `Detail` fact may follow after intervening narrative clauses. The same `badge` key occurs in multiple sections with different colors. A question asks for the badge color in one section, with the section ID sampled uniformly from completed sections. Thus the answer cannot be determined from the badge word alone or the most recent color. All next-token distributions are computed from the visible prefix; no hidden label is injected at evaluation.

The surface is still rigid: `Section N: topic` and `Detail: the badge was color` do not cover the many heading and paragraph forms in natural documents, and section IDs are sampled in random order rather than sequentially. The numbered-item FineWeb evidence motivates the *scope relation*, not these exact wordings or their frequency.

An actual generated segment is: “Section 3: home. During the election Henry saves the warm painting near the coast ... Detail: the badge was purple. Section 4: lake. Detail: the badge was green. What color was the badge in section 4? Answer: green.” The omitted narrative phrase is unrelated to the scoped fact. Exact examples and sampled seed are in [the audit JSON](surface_mixture_v7_audit.json).

The v7 audit replayed **256,000 prefixes** in 1,000 streams of 256 tokens. It checked distribution normalization, sampled-token support, independently reconstructed section facts, deterministic answers, and every alternative section key under full-prefix support. It found **1,735** scope-answer sites, **2,811** supported alternative-key edits, and **377** details separated from their headings by narrative. A held-out causal builder separately found 200/200 supported section-key edits, 123/200 supported relevant-color edits, and 120/200 supported irrelevant-color edits. The edit sets are conditional on full-prefix support, so they are not unbiased samples of all possible fact edits. A v6 regression audit passed after excluding the new clause types from narrative-history parsing.

V7 inherited generic numbered narrative lines, creating occasional double-number headings such as `3: Section 1: school`. That is a surface confound for learning the intended section key, even though the oracle and intervention checks still pass. `surface_mixture_v8.py` starts each Section heading only after a newline and rules it out within an already numbered narrative line; ordinary numbered lines remain. Its [task-rich audit](surface_mixture_v8_taskrich_audit.json) over 300 streams of 256 tokens found **429** scope-answer sites, **574** supported alternative keys, and **57** details after intervening narrative. Every Section token followed a newline. V8 is the clean follow-up for model comparisons; v7 checkpoints remain tied to the original v7 DGP.

Surface realism remains incomplete. On 192 disjoint FineWeb windows truncated to 128 GPT-2 tokens, v7 at the task-rich rates had quote/question/colon window frequencies **.344/.307/.833**, versus FineWeb **.375/.167/.328**. At lower section/query rates, v7 produced only **1** scope answer in 192 streams, and repeated-word fraction over the first 40 words remained **.270 versus .164** for FineWeb. V8's clean-line low-rate version had question and colon frequencies **.161/.479**, but only **0** scope answers in 192 streams, digit frequency **.427 versus .573**, and still no parentheses. A larger [low-rate v8 draw](surface_mixture_v8_low_rate_density.json), seed 12691, yielded only **1** scope answer in 2,000 streams of 128 tokens and **5** in 1,000 streams of 256 tokens. These are frequency probes on one development panel and arbitrary lower task rates, not a claim of natural dependency prevalence. The [v7](surface_mixture_v7_fineweb_comparison.json) and [v8](surface_mixture_v8_fineweb_comparison.json) comparisons record the matched-window settings.

The first model pilot used a two-layer, width-64, four-head, attention-only bilinear transformer with RMSNorm, 600 updates, batch 32, length 256, task-rich v7 rates, exact-oracle answer loss and supported alternate-key CE. The [held-out causal evaluation](v7_scope_pilot/causal_eval.json) found **31%** answer accuracy on 200 questions versus a **39.3%** query-blind Bayes baseline. Changing the section key changed the oracle answer by TV **1** but the model distribution by only **.0053** TV. Changing the relevant color moved the model by **.306** TV; changing an irrelevant section's color moved it by **.354** TV despite zero oracle effect. This model has not learned scoped retrieval. Its representations should not be interpreted as a recovered scope circuit. The v7 double-number confound and short training run limit architectural conclusions.

A three-layer v7 model trained for **2,000** updates with the same task-rich distribution and losses. Held-out scope-answer KL improved from **1.074** at step 1,000 to **.966** at step 2,000. On the same 200-question [causal panel](v7_scope_depth3_seed1/causal_eval.json), accuracy was **40.5%** against the **39.3%** query-blind baseline, but a section-key edit moved the model by only **.0022** TV (oracle **1**). Relevant and irrelevant fact-color edits moved it by **.432** and **.477** TV respectively (oracle **1** and **0**). This is evidence of a stronger color-based shortcut, not scoped retrieval. The small accuracy difference does not override the near-zero key effect.

The clean-format v8 three-layer bilinear model trained for **1,200** updates, batch 32 and length 256, with a higher section/question rate to recover comparable answer-site density. On its own 200-question [causal panel](v8_scope_depth3_seed1/causal_eval.json), accuracy was **47.5%** versus **46.5%** query-blind Bayes; answer CE was **.888** versus the key-blind **.779** nats. The section-key edit moved the model by **.0012** TV (oracle **1**), whereas relevant and irrelevant fact-color edits moved it by **.540** and **.541** TV (oracle **1** and **0**). Removing the double-number heading confound did not restore key use. This is a one-seed optimization result, not a general inability theorem for bilinear attention.

A same-shell softmax-attention control used the same seed, data stream, width, depth, RMSNorm, and training losses for 1,200 updates. It replaced the product-of-two-QK attention with causal Q1/K1 softmax attention; its stored model has 121,088 parameters, but Q2/K2 are inactive, leaving **96,512 active** parameters. Identically seeded bilinear and softmax models have exactly equal initial logits because attention-output weights start at zero, and a separate future-token edit left earlier softmax logits unchanged. An initial GPU run exited with code 139 before saving, while a 100-step target-shape retry and the complete run passed; the cause of that first native failure is unknown.

The control's [held-out causal panel](v8_scope_softmax_depth3_seed1/causal_eval.json) gave **46.5%** answer accuracy, equal to the **46.5%** query-blind Bayes baseline, and CE **.790** versus **.779** nats. A section-key edit moved its output by **.00030** TV (oracle **1**); relevant and irrelevant fact-color edits moved it by **.513** and **.512** TV (oracle **1** and **0**). A direct training-batch replay confirmed that the counterfactual builder edits the section-number token and assigns the exact alternative oracle answer. Both architectures favor a color-based shortcut under this setup. This control does not isolate parameter count, optimization geometry, or theoretical expressibility; it makes the shared task format/training setup a stronger suspect.

The section-specific bilinear-scaling stop criterion was section-key effect below **.10 TV** or accuracy less than five points above the query-blind baseline; v8 meets both stop conditions, and the softmax control fails the same causal gate. The next section-task diagnostic, if revisited, should be a shorter same-prefix binding curriculum with a standard-attention positive control, followed by longer-distance and mixed-rule transfer. Do not decompose these key-blind checkpoints into supposed scope circuits. The concrete featurizer deliverable, **if** a native bilinear model becomes competent on a later task or curriculum, is a matched-width comparison of weight-only and data-aware first-layer tensors and sparse bases, using held-out section keys, colors, and entire rule families; score reconstruction, task prediction, and edit fidelity separately. For now, featurizer work should use already competent multi-genre and joint-rule tasks. Rare-site conditional training should be reported against both the task-rich training DGP and a lower-rate evaluation DGP, with sufficient answer sites for confidence intervals.
