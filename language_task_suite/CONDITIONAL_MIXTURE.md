# A conditional toy language for causal rule-mixture tests

The [original toy-language proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) calls for knowing how language primitives interfere in a single joint DGP. The [weight sweep](MIXED_RULE_WEIGHT_SWEEP.md) varied bigram versus induction probabilities, but its separately trained models saw different context distributions. Here one model receives a special **mixture tag** before the stream. The stream itself is generated by the original Python `LanguageGenerator`; the tag is external conditioning information and is excluded from its rule context. On a fixed text prefix, changing only the tag is a causal intervention inside the same network.

## Data and training

Three tags specify the baseline, bigram-heavy, and induction-heavy weights from the sweep. The 26-token stream vocabulary becomes 29 including tags. `train_conditional_mixed_rules.py` trains a two-layer, four-head, width-64, attention-only bilinear transformer for 10,000 CPU steps, batch size 64, with equal interleaved exposure to the three mixtures. The model sees 64 generated tokens after the tag. The next-token oracle remains the exact prefix-conditioned distribution of the Python generator, augmented by zero tag probability and a uniform first generated token.

On 256 independently generated test sequences per mixture, conflict-context oracle KL in nats/token was:

| Step | Baseline | Bigram-heavy | Induction-heavy |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2,000 | 0.082 | 0.060 | 0.128 |
| 6,000 | 0.046 | 0.034 | 0.073 |
| 10,000 | 0.046 | 0.040 | 0.059 |

These are one-model, one-seed results; convergence is not monotone. The saved checkpoint and exact figures are in `conditional_mixed_rules_v1/results.json`.

## Fixed-prefix mixture intervention

`eval_mixed_rule_fixed_context_shift.py` takes the same 520 baseline-generated bigram–induction conflict prefixes used in the prior sweep, reruns this **one model** with each tag, and measures the change in the log odds of the two proposed tokens. For baseline→induction-heavy, the exact oracle shift has mean **−1.441**, SD **0.214**. The conditional model produces mean **−1.537**, SD **0.450**, and RMSE to the oracle shift **0.432**. The separately trained width-64 models had RMSE **1.188**. For baseline→bigram-heavy, the conditional model's shift RMSE is **0.292** versus **1.348** for separately trained width-64 models. The conditional model is substantially more faithful, though its context-dependent variation still exceeds the oracle's. These are behavioral comparisons, not evidence that the separately trained models share aligned parameters.

## Exact structural bound at the first attention bond

For an ordinary query position `t > 0` in the **first** attention layer, two inputs differ only in the mixture-tag embedding at position 0. Their query vectors at `t` and every non-tag key/value vector are identical. With the model's unnormalized squared bilinear attention, each head's pre-output-projection contrast is therefore

\[
\Delta y_{h,t}
=\alpha^{(D)}_{h,t}v_h(\text{tag}_D)
-\alpha^{(R)}_{h,t}v_h(\text{tag}_R),
\]

where each `alpha` is the scalar attention score from query `t` to the tag. Across **all** prefixes and ordinary positions, this contrast lies in the span of the two fixed tag-value vectors for each head. For `H` heads, its rank is at most `2H`, here **8**. RoPE changes the scalar scores, not the span of tag values. The bound does not apply at the tag's own query position, nor to deeper layers after the tag signal has altered ordinary residual states.

`eval_conditional_bond_mediation.py` verifies this numerically. Fit on 128 separate baseline-generated sequences, using **only no-conflict ordinary positions** and excluding the tag position. The first-bond tag contrast is numerically rank eight: its top 2/4/8 singular directions contain **96.1% / 98.7% / approximately 100%** of squared contrast energy. Head 2 carries 62.2% and head 3 carries 34.2% of raw contrast energy. The second attention bond is much less compact: top 4/8/16 directions contain 59.7% / 78.0% / 91.2%, consistent with subsequent mixing.

## Causal bond mediation, not just reconstruction

For each held-out prefix, we run the same model under baseline and induction-heavy tags. We then patch selected coordinates of the induction-heavy **donor's** pre-output-projection activation into the baseline **recipient's** first attention bond, leaving the rest of the recipient computation intact. The score is RMSE of patched versus donor *bigram-minus-induction log-odds shift* on exactly bigram+induction conflict positions. A no-patch recipient has RMSE **1.415** on the first test set. Full-width patching at ordinary positions lowers it to **0.579**, so this bond mediates about **83% of the squared donor–recipient shift energy**. The remaining error is carried by unpatched paths, including the residual stream and later computation; full patching is not expected to reproduce the donor exactly.

| First-bond patch basis | Coordinates | Conflict shift RMSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Physical axes | 4 | 1.412 |
| Random orthogonal | 4 | 1.337 |
| Activation PCA | 4 | 1.414 |
| Weight-only SVD | 4 | 1.374 |
| **Tag-contrast PCA**, fit without conflicts | **4** | **0.582** |
| All bases, full-width | 64 | 0.579 |

The four-direction result repeats at RMSE **0.586** on a second test set with **1,713** held-out conflict positions (versus 796 in the first); its full-width RMSE is **0.584**. Patching the tag position alone has no measurable mediation (full-width RMSE **1.416**); patching only ordinary positions reproduces the all-position effect. This localizes the compact contrast to the signal broadcast from the tag to later queries through first-layer attention. A full 16-coordinate patch of head 2 alone gives RMSE **0.913**, head 3 alone **1.155**; the four-dimensional contrast basis combines the relevant head contributions.

For a selectivity control, we identified **6,160** held-out no-conflict positions where the exact oracle distribution is unchanged (within 1e-6) by the tag change. The model's full-tag KL on these positions is only **0.00147** nats/token. The four-direction patch induces **0.00061** KL from the baseline model on the same positions, comparable to full-width patching through this bond (**0.00064**). Thus the compact patch is not simply perturbing all predictions indiscriminately. The replicated test set has 12,237 such oracle-stable positions and gives similar values.

This is a strong example of a **supervised, causal prior** yielding a compact local basis that transfers from no-conflict fit contexts to withheld rule conflicts. Its rank-eight part is enforced by the architecture and the single changed tag key/value, so the rank bound itself is not evidence that the model discovered a four-dimensional language primitive. The empirical result is that four of those eight directions account for nearly all of this bond's *behavioral* mediation on the tested conflicts. It does not establish a globally canonical attention decomposition, a unique gauge, or recovery of the full DGP mechanism. The 26-token stream remains deliberately diagnostic rather than FineWeb-like.

Next: test whether the same basis transfers to other changed rule classes and larger, more natural vocabularies; include matched token/context marginals and whole-rule-family holdouts. Compare this supervised contrast basis to sparsity and function-aware bases at matched mediation and oracle fidelity, and track how the rank expands across layers. The toy's verified joint DGP supplies labels for both training and validation without treating rule labels as proof of mechanistic recovery.
