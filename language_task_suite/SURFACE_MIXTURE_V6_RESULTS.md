# Source-attributed claims in the joint toy language (v6)

The [original proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) asks for a toy language where known rules compete, so that a proposed circuit can be checked against the actual data-generating process. `surface_mixture_v6.py` retains the v5 narrative with four-way verb and object mixtures and counterbalanced object-place questions, and interleaves **speaker-attributed claims** in the same autoregressive stream. The source task was chosen after the [inspected FineWeb cue panel](FINEWEB_DEPENDENCY_PANEL.md) found clearer local attribution and numbered-item scope than the broad correction and arithmetic regexes. The panel does not establish natural task prevalence.

An actual generated segment is: “As Bob said, ‘the coat was missing’. As Grace said, ‘the letter was missing’. As Grace said, ‘the camera was missing’. As Dave said, ‘the box was missing’. Who said the coat was missing? Answer: Bob.” Narrative clauses appear in the surrounding stream. The source of each claim object is sampled independently from eight speakers, and objects are not reused in source statements. The query object is uniform among observed claim objects, and the source question is available only after at least two speakers appear. Thus the visible query key can change the correct answer; a query-blind constant is not an exact solution. Source statements and questions are excluded from the original narrative-history rules, so they do not silently redefine the verb/object oracle.

`audit_surface_mixture_v6.py` replayed **384,000 prefixes** in 3,000 streams of 128 tokens and checked normalization, sampled-token support, complete statement/question grammar, independently replayed source and place answers, every narrative verb/object mixture against the standalone v3 oracle, and uniform source-query keys with alternative speakers. All checks passed. The task-rich stream had **15,226 verb**, **14,968 object**, **395 place-answer**, and **1,555 source-answer** sites. `surface_mixture_v6_audit.json` records the exact configuration and representative generated text. The training-time alternative-query targets were separately checked for full-prefix support and correct changed answers.

The realism check remains negative. On the frozen 480-window FineWeb panel, using 128-token windows, synthetic versus FineWeb occurrence rates are **86.5% versus 34.4%** for quotes, **38.8% versus 21.9%** for question marks, **63.5% versus 27.9%** for colons, and **0% versus 35.2%** for parentheses. Among 479 windows with at least 40 alphabetic words in both streams, the repeated-word fraction over the first 40 words is **28.0% synthetic versus 16.7% FineWeb**. Different tokenizers and sampled task-rich rates limit direct comparison, but the gap is too large for a FineWeb-like claim. A separate [500-stream rate check](surface_mixture_v6_ratecheck.json) at quote/source rates .15/.25 reduced the quote-window rate to **62.3%** and question-window rate to **24.6%**, but source-answer coverage fell to **64 sites in 500 streams**; this is a data-density versus surface-calibration tradeoff, not yet a calibrated DGP.

The first model test trains **one seed** of a two-layer, width-64, four-head, attention-only bilinear transformer with RMSNorm for 3,000 updates at batch 64 and length 128. The loss combines sampled next-token CE with exact-oracle soft targets at verb/object/place/source sites and one supported alternative query-object target per source-question example. The alternative examples are constructed independently: if a sequence contains several source questions, changing an earlier query does not leave its old answer in a later training prefix. This training method is causal supervision for the model, not evidence of a causal circuit by itself. The current result does not isolate depth, width, or the effect of counterfactual supervision because it lacks matched model controls.

The run finished with held-out seed-8101 oracle KL **.200** at verbs, **.403** at objects, **2.208** at place answers, and **.989** at source answers. These sites have very different conditional entropies and frequencies, so the values should not be averaged into a single competence claim. On the separate seed-9101 source panel, the model reaches **47.3%** answer accuracy and **.977 nats** answer CE, compared with the query-blind Bayes **47.2%** and **.831 nats**. The gap from the Bayes CE is evidence that the model is not even a fully calibrated query-blind predictor.

The decisive evaluation uses a held-out seed-9101 stream and three full-prefix-supported paired edits: change the queried claim object, change the attributed speaker of that claim, and change another claim's speaker as a no-effect control. In 3,000 streams it finds **1,523** source questions and **1,523/1,237/1,351** supported query-object/relevant-speaker/other-speaker edit pairs. The best answer distribution that ignores the query object has Bayes accuracy **47.2%** and answer CE **.831 nats** on these histories; copying the first or latest source speaker gives **46.6%** or **42.7%** accuracy. These are model-free baselines. A bond decomposition should be attempted only after the native model demonstrates source-key sensitivity; otherwise it can faithfully compress an answer shortcut just as in v5. A further test should hold out whole claim-object/speaker combinations or one of several source-statement surface forms during featurizer fitting, and compare weight-only with data-aware bases at matched dimension, model fidelity, and oracle-effect fidelity.

| Supported edit | Oracle effect TV | Model effect TV | Effect L1 error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Change queried claim object | 1 | **.0024** | 2.001 |
| Change that claim's speaker | 1 | .478 | 1.303 |
| Change another claim's speaker | 0 | **.501** | 1.001 |

The model is sensitive to speaker names but essentially **blind to which claim is queried**. It reacts almost as much to an unrelated speaker as to the correct one. Changing a first or last quoted speaker has a larger effect (roughly .48–.54 TV) than changing a middle one (roughly .34–.37). Its top prediction equals the first quoted speaker on **60.8%** of source questions. These observations are consistent with a position-weighted aggregation or copying shortcut; they do not uniquely identify its internal algorithm. The source-specific sweep stops at this checkpoint for interpretation work. A deeper or better-balanced model is worth testing for competence, but decomposing this checkpoint into “source-binding features” would be misleading.

The failure also persists when evaluation uses the lower quote/source rates .15/.25. Among **373** source questions in 3,000 fresh streams, model accuracy is **48.3%** versus a query-blind Bayes **48.8%**; query-object edit TV is **.0026** (oracle **1**) and unrelated-speaker edit TV **.531** (oracle **0**). This is a distribution-frequency shift, not a held-out rule family or a natural-text result.

There is nevertheless an exact **weight-only functional object** for its first layer. For head (h), relative lag Δ, query token (a), key token (b), and residual-output coordinate (o), it is (T^h_{Δ}(a,b,o)=S^h_{Δ}(a,b)P^h(b,o)), where (S) is the product of both RoPE-adjusted QK scores and (P) folds V into the head's output slice. `eval_v6_first_layer_token_tensor.py` includes embeddings and per-token RMSNorm in these factors. Summing its contributions reproduces native first-layer outputs on eight fresh 48-token streams with maximum absolute error **1.43e-6** and mean relative L2 error **2.09e-7**. At relative lags 0, 1, 4 and 16, the top 16 singular directions of each head's token-pair score matrix explain **97.1–99.9%** of squared score energy. Depending on head and lag, **12–52%** of score energy comes from pairs where *both* QK factors are negative, yielding a positive product. This concretely illustrates the original proposal's warning about factor-sign interpretation. The tensor is exact for **layer zero**, but its SVD is not an end-to-end canonical source circuit, and high score-energy concentration says nothing by itself about the failed query-key effect.
