# Nested brackets in the joint-rule language stream

This versioned task adds bracket closures to the existing six-frame verb stream, preserving its competing lexical, induction, previous-place, and cue rules. It is a controlled dependency task, not a claim that its bracket density or prose resembles web text. Source: `surface_mixture_v2_nested.py` (fixed filler length) and `surface_mixture_v2_nested_varlen.py` (one or two filler clauses per span).

## Exact data-generating process and checks

Each inserted tail opens `(` or `[`, optionally opens another span, and later requires its matching `)` or `]`. The choice of opener is independent of the filler words. The generator returns the exact next-token distribution from every visible prefix, so both next-token KL and grammar-valid causal edits have an oracle. The variable-length successor gives outer-close opener distances 10, 14, or 18 tokens. Its deterministic inner closes have distance 8; optional close sites are separate and are not counted in the deterministic-close edit test.

The 1,000-stream, 128-token variable-length audit checked all 128,000 prefixes for normalized oracle distributions and sampled support. It found 6,410 verb sites, 489 deterministic inner closes, 981 deterministic outer closes, and outer-close distances 10:246, 14:499, 18:236. The bracket stack independently parsed from the sampled prefix always agreed with the deterministic oracle. Relevant opener edits changed the required closer; edits to the other opener or a filler word did not. The original verb oracle was unchanged at every verb site. The fixed-length predecessor passed the same checks, but its deterministic close positions had fixed lags 4 and 10 and therefore permitted a simple position shortcut. See `surface_mixture_v2_nested_varlen_audit.json` and `surface_mixture_v2_nested_audit.json`.

## Model and causal results

A one-block, four-head, width-64 bilinear attention-only transformer with RMSNorm was trained for 1,500 steps on length-128 streams (batch 32, exact-oracle verb and nested-close auxiliary losses, weight 1 each). On its independent 256-stream evaluation panel, the variable-length model reached verb KL **0.2714**, induction-verb KL **0.3050**, deterministic inner-close KL **0.0020**, and outer-close KL **0.0005**. These are next-token KLs to the exact generator, not reconstruction or circuit-fidelity metrics. The fixed-length predecessor reached verb KL 0.2469, inner-close KL 0.0008, and outer-close KL 0.0009 at the same step count.

An independent 512-stream variable-length causal panel (seed 20261203) supplied 254 deterministic inner and 499 deterministic outer close sites. The model predicted the correct closer after **100%** of the relevant and control edits. Its probability-change total variation under relevant opener edits was 0.9983 (inner) and 0.9990 (outer), against oracle 1.0; under control edits it was 0.0014 and 0.0002. Mean L1 error of the full probability-change vector was 0.0065 (inner relevant), 0.0028 (inner control), 0.0026 (outer relevant), and 0.0003 (outer control). The outer relevant L1 remained low at each distance: 0.0027 at lag 10 (121 pairs), 0.0013 at lag 14 (251), and 0.0048 at lag 18 (127). Raw metrics and per-distance values are in `surface_mixture_v2_nested_varlen_depth1/causal_edits_seed20261203.json`.

These edits establish that the model uses the opener token on this distribution, rather than only the unchanged filler. They do **not** establish a general stack algorithm: nesting is capped at two, the grammar is predictable, and the model could learn a small collection of relative-position cases. A stronger transfer test should vary lengths beyond training support, nesting depth, filler grammar, and intervening distractors while preserving a correct exact oracle.

## Realism and interpretation status

The frozen 480-row FineWeb development-panel form comparison is negative. In 128-token windows, the variable-length synthetic stream has 311 distinct alphabetic words versus 8,616 in the sampled FineWeb text; 69.4% of synthetic windows contain square brackets versus 1.46% of FineWeb windows, and no synthetic windows contain newlines versus 86.0% of FineWeb windows. Tokenizers and windows differ, so these are coarse form checks, not prevalence estimates. The task is useful as ground truth for a conditional opener-to-closer circuit; it is not a realistic language sample. See `surface_mixture_v2_nested_varlen_fineweb_form.json`.

The next interpretability experiment is to identify and causally test the opener-to-close computation in this competent model, then compare its weight-only first-layer token-space reduction with distribution-weighted and sparsity-optimized variants at matched task KL and opener-edit fidelity. Keep verb-rule effects separate: a sparse basis that recovers bracket closing while damaging the existing competing verb computation is not a successful joint explanation. The present one-block model is a capability gate for that comparison, not evidence of a canonical or sparse circuit.
