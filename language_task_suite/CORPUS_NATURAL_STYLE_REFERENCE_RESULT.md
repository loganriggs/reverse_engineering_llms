# Natural spelling-style cues in the unedited rule reference stream

The [joint FineWeb reference stream](corpus_rule_reference_dgp.py) now tags adjacent **different-family** US/UK spelling words separated by 20–300 words, alongside numbered-list continuation, bracket closing, and repeated three-letter codes. These style sites come from unchanged source documents. Both agreeing and disagreeing spelling pairs are retained: selecting only agreeing pairs would manufacture the appearance of a reliable rule. A local site requires the earlier cue and later answer to be single BPE tokens in the same ordinary nonoverlapping chunk. All valid next-token positions, including unlabelled background, remain training targets.

The [audit](audit_corpus_rule_reference_dgp.py) used two disjoint, source-unconditioned 5,000-document FineWeb `sample-10BT` panels (offsets 413,000–417,999 and 418,000–422,999), with 512-token chunks and at most 2,048 source tokens per document. List and bracket transformations were identity, nonce editing was disabled, and literal-code/style rates enabled tagging only. No document text was modified. The [fixture](check_corpus_rule_reference_dgp.py) verifies both matching and disagreeing spelling pairs, local BPE visibility, and the earlier three-family composition case.

The majority baseline takes the more frequent US/UK form **separately for each target lexical family on the evaluation panel itself**, so it is an optimistic descriptive baseline, not a held-out fitted predictor. The cue beats it by 11.1 and 4.2 percentage points in the two panels. The cue/target contingency tables are 411 US→US, 35 US→UK, 29 UK→US, 108 UK→UK in panel one; 416, 44, 46, 91 in panel two. The association is real in these samples but imperfect; a matching cue is not an exact next-word oracle. Earlier cues and targets may also share topic, source site, or editorial style. Manual review found ordinary prose examples such as `favorite` before `color` and `cancelling` before `colour`, along with scraped pages whose apparent document boundaries or authorship are less clear.

This rule family is useful for the next model experiment because it adds a document-level statistical dependency with natural wording and co-occurrence with previously tagged local mechanisms. It is **not** yet a learned capability or a causally identified style circuit. The next gate should train on unchanged, unselected FineWeb chunks with an explicitly reported site-weight schedule, hold out whole source documents and lexical-family combinations, and compare later-spelling likelihood against target-family priors and source-cue edits. Only after a model behaviorally uses the cue should canonicalization or sparsity be evaluated for a style component. Report full-token CE, site prediction, cue intervention response, and background KL separately; keep list, bracket, and code gates independent.
