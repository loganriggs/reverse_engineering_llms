# Reconnect the language benchmark to the existing mixed-rule generator

The user's [project proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) states the organizing loop: build a toy LLM from known computational primitives, use it to study phenomena and discover better interpretation tools, then feed the new primitives back into the language. The explicit appeal of the toy is knowing the *joint* DGP, including how bigram statistics, skip rules, and induction interfere. This is a stronger and more relevant target than merely adding individually labeled prompt families.

The earlier toy-language work was already implemented in a **different local repository**, [`/home/loganriggs/Coding/toy_models_of_tensor_networks/attn_circuits`](../../toy_models_of_tensor_networks/attn_circuits). The first survey concentrated on `tensor_language` and its natural-text experiments and missed this directory. That was a research-direction error: the new 12-family prompts and document worlds are useful for semantic state tracking, but they do not replace a generative language model whose rule distributions interfere at every token.

## Existing assets worth reusing

- `generator.py` implements autoregressive mixed and isolated streams with token bigrams, token trigrams, skip-bigrams, induction, brackets, configurable class weights, and per-token rule labels. Shared output tokens let different rules favor the same next token. Its weights are normalized **among the candidates currently active**; setup tokens and whole bracket spans follow additional sampling paths. It is therefore not literally a fixed unconditional weighted sum of rule distributions at every position.
- `generator_v3.py` has a more readable category-based stream: probabilistic noun bigrams (70/20/10), category transitions, token/category trigrams, skip-bigrams, noun induction, and bracket/quote spans. Here rule dispatch is **priority-based**, not the earlier sampled mixture. It retains stochastic distributions within rules. The label `skip_trigram` denotes a bracket closer, not a general skip-trigram grammar.
- `train.py`, `eval_rules.py`, `model.py`, saved configurations/checkpoints, and circuit analyses already support and evaluate these generators. For example, `models/bilinear_gpt_v3_all_2L_4H_d64_5000steps.pt` is a two-attention-layer, 64-dimensional, no-MLP checkpoint. Its saved per-rule held-out CE includes noun bigram 0.890, induction 0.124, skip-bigram 0.073, token trigram 0.086, and bracket close 0.605 nats. These are **not** directly comparable across rule types without their true conditional entropies; the saved entropy estimates for some stochastic labels are approximate.

`audit_existing_mixture.py` sampled 1,000 × 128 tokens from each generator. In the original mixed generator, 54,639 positions were labeled as one of the four ordinary rules. Among these, 21,103 had one active candidate, 23,374 had two, and 10,162 had three. **37.9% of rule-labeled positions had multiple active candidates proposing different outputs.** This is the concrete collision/interference regime the newer document-world work omitted. See `existing_mixture_audit.json`.

A direct conditional-probability check used context `a B0 a`: the bigram proposes `X0`, while induction proposes `B0`. Their configured weights imply P(`X0`) = 0.25/(0.25+0.20) = 0.556. Across 20,000 draws, the generator emitted `X0` with frequency 0.564. This checks one collision branch, not the full generator oracle.

## Exact observable oracle and saved-model baseline

`mixed_rule_oracle.py` now gives the complete next-token distribution for the original **Python** `LanguageGenerator` conditioned on the visible prefix. It includes active-rule weight normalization, the setup path, the bracket-start decision, and a forward filter over the hidden bracket rule and remaining span length. The controlled `a B0 a` context above tests a local dispatch branch but is not naturally reachable from sequence start. In contrast, the naturally reachable prefix `g d` makes a bigram and trigram compete: the exact observable oracle predicts P(`B0`) = 0.5266, P(`X0`) = 0.4634, and P(`(`) = 0.0100 at total length 8; 20,000 full-sequence samples have total-variation distance 0.0039. A separate ambiguous-parenthesis-prefix check has TV 0.0107. See `verify_mixed_rule_oracle.py` and `mixed_rule_oracle_checks.json`.

`eval_existing_mixed_oracle.py` scores saved original models against that exact distribution, not merely sampled next-token loss. On the same 1,024 freshly generated 64-token sequences, the saved 1-layer and 2-layer attention-only checkpoints have overall KLs of **0.0420** and **0.0246** nats/token respectively. On visible contexts where active rule candidates disagree, KLs are **0.1420** and **0.0813**; on no-conflict contexts, **0.0213** and **0.0129**. The conflict group is defined from visible context, regardless of whether a hidden bracket span currently governs the sampled token. These scores are exploratory because the saved configs do not identify whether training used the Python or separate fast generator, or which source revision. See `existing_mixed_model_oracle_1layer.json` and `existing_mixed_model_oracle_2layer.json`.

`eval_existing_mixed_bond_gauges.py` then tests a local attention-output bond of the saved 2-layer model. It fits activation PCA on 128 generated sequences and evaluates on another 128. Physical axes, seeded random axes, activation PCA, and a **weight-only** right-singular-vector basis are all full-width orthogonal gauges: at 64/64 coordinates, their logits match the original within numerical tolerance. Truncating the first attention bond to 16/64 coordinates gives conflict-context oracle KL **0.996** on physical axes, **0.692** on a random basis, **0.153** on activation PCA, and **0.612** on weight SVD, versus **0.080** untruncated. For the second bond, the same values are **0.751**, **0.609**, **0.121**, and **0.124**. At 32/64 in the second bond, weight SVD yields **0.081** and activation PCA **0.084**. See `existing_mixed_bond_gauges_layer0.json` and `existing_mixed_bond_gauges_layer1.json` for all widths, no-conflict results, model-to-truncation KL, and bond reconstruction error.

This shows that the raw coordinate system is a poor *compression* basis and that a simple weight-only basis is competitive for the later bond. PCA minimizing activation error is expected to beat raw axes for low-dimensional reconstruction. None of these results identifies a unique gauge, a canonical attention function, or a rule-specific causal component. The next decisive experiment must fit a basis without a held-out rule-conflict pattern, then test whether its components predict and selectively mediate changes in the competing-rule conditional distribution. A rule-weight intervention and a matched marginal-token control would be stronger than another IID compression score.

A first transfer check fit activation PCA **only on no-conflict positions** from the same 128 fitting sequences, while holding the evaluation sequences fixed. At 16/64 coordinates, first-bond conflict KL rose from **0.153** to **0.219** and second-bond conflict KL from **0.121** to **0.214**. At 32/64, these became **0.094** and **0.087** (from **0.090** and **0.084**). The weight-only SVD basis is unchanged by this fit restriction; at second-bond 16/64 it achieves **0.124**. This suggests that conflict examples matter substantially for a very compact activation basis, while a somewhat wider basis generalizes better. It does **not** hold out an entire rule family, control marginal token frequencies, or establish rule localization. See `existing_mixed_bond_gauges_layer0_fit_no_conflict.json` and `existing_mixed_bond_gauges_layer1_fit_no_conflict.json`.

## Corrected experimental role

The mixed-rule generator should be the **primary sandbox for competition and shared computation**: vary the bigram distribution and induction/skip/bracket prevalence, measure per-rule held-out CE or exact KL, then ask whether sparse/canonical attention coordinates recover the planted rules and their conflicts. The document-world generator is a complementary stress test for longer context, state updates, and cross-sentence binding. It should be added as another task distribution only after the mixed-rule model's own conditional probabilities and evaluation are audited.

Immediate tests, in order:

1. Sweep rule collisions by varying configured weights and shared-output assignments while holding marginal token counts and model size as stable as possible. Train ordinary full next-token models; report per-rule CE/KL, not only answer accuracy. Include isolated-rule and no-conflict controls.
2. Use the exact oracle and saved checkpoints to test sparse/intervention-aware decompositions on held-out conflict patterns. A component should predict the *change* when a rule's weight or trigger is altered, including cases where another rule overrides it. The local bond compression result above is a baseline, not this test.
3. Extend to 1–4 bilinear blocks with MLPs, then test transfer to the document-world tasks and natural-text cue classes. Keep whole-rule-family and conflict-pattern holdouts distinct from ordinary token holdouts.

The v3 surface stream is only loosely English-like, and the document world is templated. Neither has yet passed a meaningful natural-text similarity check. FineWeb should calibrate frequencies and contexts, while the exact synthetic rules remain separate ground truth.
