# Parenthetical aliases inside the joint-rule language

The [random FineWeb form panel](FINEWEB_RANDOM_FORM_PANEL.md) identified parenthetical structure as a conspicuous missing form in the readable toy languages. It did not establish that alias definitions are common. The new [alias generator](surface_alias_mixture.py) tests a specific computational hypothesis from the [original proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages): a later subject may require resolving an earlier name definition **before** the already sampled lexical and induction rules compete for the next verb.

The generator extends the six-frame event stream with parenthetical clauses such as `Nadia (also called Dina).` and `Hannah (known as Ivo).` A matched `Quinn (a local colleague).` aside introduces the same punctuation without alias semantics. Alias tokens are assigned to people anew in each stream; they can subsequently appear as subjects or in other person slots. At a verb slot after an alias subject, the oracle maps the alias to its visible referent and calculates the original 70/20/10 lexical proposal, previous-subject induction proposal when available, frame cue, and previous-place proposal. The rule weights are still normalized over active proposals. The oracle is a pure function of the visible prefix; there is no unobserved alias table at evaluation.

## Generator audit and density

[The replay audit](audit_surface_alias_mixture.py) checks normalization and sampled-token support at every prefix, replays the stored distribution, and independently recomputes the weighted mixture at each verb. On **1,000 streams × 128 tokens** at the task-rich rates, all checks pass. There are **752** alias-definition sites, **688** alias-subject verb sites, **351** of those with an induction proposal, and **680** with disagreement among modal rule proposals. The audit constructs **141** alias-key edits with complete edited-prefix support and a changed current-verb oracle; their mean oracle total variation is **.512**. The definitions span **460** distinct alias-person pairs, with each alias attached to 25–33 different people in this sample; no alias has one fixed referent. The audit also verifies the relevant alias and canonical person in each sampled context. Results: [task-rich JSON](surface_alias_audit.json).

At a lower parenthetical/definition/reuse setting chosen on the FineWeb **development** sample, the same 1,000 × 128-token audit finds **265** alias definitions, **131** alias-subject verb sites, and only **9** supported alternative-alias key edits. The 32-window form pilot has parentheses in **12/32** low-rate toy samples versus **11/32** natural excerpts; this rate was selected after seeing that pilot. A frozen-rate check on a separate panel gives **61/192** alias-stream windows with parentheses versus **63/192** natural windows. This validates one coarse frequency only. The low-rate stream still lacks natural document layout, quotations, questions, colons and numbers. It is a density control, not a realistic language distribution. See [low-rate audit](surface_alias_low_rate_audit.json) and [held-out form counts](alias_surface_holdout.json).

The [paired-edit evaluator](eval_surface_alias_edits.py) constructs three supported interventions from fresh generated streams: replace an alias subject by its **canonical name**, which must leave the oracle verb distribution exactly unchanged; replace it by another defined alias with a different referent; and change the **earlier definition's referent** while keeping the later alias token unchanged. The last test directly asks whether the model reads the definition rather than treating the alias token as a fixed lexical identity. The evaluator separately reports first post-definition uses, before previous alias uses can serve as shortcuts. Its test examples are newly seeded, but the pilot model was trained with all alias-person pairings; whole-pair training holdouts remain a future gate.

A later negative control changes an **irrelevant alias's** earlier definition while keeping the current alias subject fixed and retaining only fully supported edits for which the exact verb oracle is unchanged. On panel A/B there are **168/167** such edits. The bilinear pilot changes its predicted distribution by TV **.110/.111**; the 3,000-update standard-attention control changes it by **.191/.178**. Thus even the stronger definition response of the standard-attention model is partly sensitive to a definition that should not affect this prediction. This control does not by itself identify whether the shortcut is recency, position, or a pooled definition representation.

## Model pilot: partially reads definitions, fails name equivalence

A two-layer width-64, four-head bilinear transformer with RMSNorm trained for **1,500** updates on 127-token contexts at task-rich alias rates, using ordinary next-token CE plus one extra exact-oracle expected-CE term on verb slots. On 256 held-out training-DGP streams its overall verb KL is **.225** and induction-active verb KL **.286** nats. These aggregate scores do not establish alias competence. The [paired-edit evaluator](eval_surface_alias_edits.py) used two independent fresh panels of 1,024 streams each. Its initial edit builder picked the first supported replacement person, overrepresenting `Alice` in definition edits (**450/581** on the first panel). We therefore rotated the candidate order deterministically by stream and position before scoring both models; the balanced panel's 581 definition edits use **40** distinct replacement people. Table entries below use this balanced policy and report mean L1 error between the model's and exact oracle's *probability change* at the current verb.

| Edit | Panel 20261106 | Panel 20261107 | Error from predicting no change, panels A/B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alias → its canonical name, oracle effect zero | **.646** (777) | **.656** (858) | 0 / 0 |
| Alias → another defined alias | **1.145** (157) | **1.068** (154) | 1.089 / 1.020 |
| Change the earlier definition's referent | **.773** (581) | **.779** (634) | 1.013 / .992 |

The model's mean TV under the alias-to-canonical *no-effect* edit is **.323/.328**. Under the different-alias edit, its TV is only **.080/.075** versus the oracle's **.545/.510**, with near-zero mean effect-direction cosine. Yet changing the earlier definition's referent moves the model more substantially (TV **.219/.199** versus oracle **.506/.496**) and in roughly the right direction (cosine **.757/.710**). First post-definition uses show the same pattern; it is not explained solely by repeated later uses of the alias. The originally biased and balanced edit panels give the same qualitative result, but only the balanced panel addresses the replacement-person confound. These results indicate partial sensitivity to the definition but failure to implement an alias as an interchangeable name for its referent. They are not grounds for decomposing a recovered alias circuit.

The pilot used **all alias-person pairings** during training. Fresh streams are independent evaluations, but not whole-pair training holdouts. It is also possible that 1,500 updates and ordinary verb soft targets underweight the binding operation. A matched causal-supervision run therefore added exact-oracle CE on supported alias-to-canonical, alternative-alias, and definition-referent edited prefixes. It kept the model architecture, initialization, ordinary training streams, and update count fixed, using 1,024 separate causal training streams and eight examples per edit type per update. The additional loss weight was one. This is an optimization and supervision diagnostic, not a new DGP.

The first causal run is **negative**. On the same held-out training-DGP streams, overall/induction-active verb KL worsens from **.225/.286** to **.303/.390** nats. On the two balanced edit panels, effect L1 errors change as follows:

| Edit | Baseline A/B | First-choice causal training A/B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Alias → canonical, oracle effect zero | .646 / .656 | **.739 / .748** |
| Alias → another alias | 1.145 / 1.068 | **1.208 / 1.129** |
| Change earlier definition's referent | .773 / .779 | **.890 / .884** |

The first-choice causal training cache overrepresents early names as replacement referents; its edit target distribution differs from the balanced evaluation. But the causal model is also slightly worse on the *matching first-choice* test policy: definition-edit errors are **.783/.791** versus baseline **.767/.773**. Thus a selection bias is not the only problem. A second run changed **only** the training edit-selection policy to deterministically rotate replacement names and alias keys. Its overall/induction-active verb KL is **.327/.416**, also worse than the baseline. On balanced test panels A/B, effect L1 errors are **.720/.732** for alias-to-canonical, **1.249/1.127** for alternate aliases, and **.885/.877** for changed definitions. All are worse than the baseline; balancing the intervention pool did not rescue the auxiliary loss. These two intervention-trained models should not be used as evidence of an alias circuit.

The standard-attention control trained for **3,000** updates on the same task-rich stream reaches overall/induction-active verb KL **.144/.219**. It is a capability test, not a parameter-matched architecture comparison: its inactive second QK branch gives it fewer active attention parameters, and the original bilinear pilot had only 1,500 updates. On the same two balanced edit panels, its effect L1 errors are **.407/.400** for alias-to-canonical, **1.106/1.056** for alternate aliases, and **.575/.564** for changed definitions. The model's definition-edit TV **.471/.468** is close to the oracle **.506/.496**, with effect-direction cosine **.850/.830**. But alternate-alias TV is only **.050/.058** versus the oracle **.545/.510**, and the identity-preserving alias-to-canonical edit still changes predictions by TV **.203/.200**. Thus ordinary attention clearly learns something about the definition and still does not implement the full equivalence relation or key substitution. This is not a successful capability positive control.

The bilinear model trained for the same **3,000** updates reaches overall/induction-active verb KL **.168/.229**. On balanced panels A/B, effect L1 errors are **.541/.537** for alias-to-canonical, **1.139/1.049** for alternate aliases, and **.615/.620** for changed definitions. Its irrelevant-definition TV is **.168/.165**. Longer training improves its definition response and aggregate KL versus the 1,500-update pilot, but does not fix the alternate-alias failure. The 3,000-update softmax control has somewhat better aggregate KL and definition edits; both architectures exhibit the same qualitative missing key-substitution behavior. These are single training seeds, so they establish a diagnostic failure in these checkpoints, not an architecture-level ranking.

The next competence gate is a shorter binding curriculum that retains the lexical/induction collision but increases the fraction of contexts with two defined aliases and evaluates whole alias-person combination holdouts. Only after a model follows identity-preserving and referent-changing edits should we test whether a weight-only or data-aware tensor basis recovers the binding computation. Further causal-loss weight sweeps are paused.

## Reproduction

Use `/home/loganriggs/Coding/neuron_circuits/.venv/bin/python` from the repository root. `audit_surface_alias_mixture.py` checks the task-rich DGP and accepts the lower rates via `--parenthetical-rate .25 --definition-start-rate .10 --alias-reuse-rate .10`. `train_surface_mixture.py --generator alias --depths 2 --steps 1500 --batch 32 --length 127 --neval 256 --rmsnorm --verb-soft-weight 1 --device cuda --seed 0 --output-dir ...` produces the ordinary pilot. Adding `--alias-causal-weight 1 --alias-causal-nstreams 1024 --alias-causal-per-kind 8` reproduces the intervention loss; `--alias-causal-edit-selection balanced` selects the second training policy. `eval_surface_alias_edits.py --model-dir ... --depth 2 --n 1024 --length 127 --seed 20261106 --edit-selection balanced --output ...` scores a held-out panel. Repeat with seed `20261107`. The evaluator now also includes the irrelevant-definition control; earlier JSON panels predate it and their first three edit scores are unchanged. Model directories retain `results.json`, the checkpoint, and panel JSONs; the generator, model, and test seeds are recorded there.
