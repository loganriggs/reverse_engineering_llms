# Aligning the language benchmark with the original proposal

The [proposal itself](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) specifies the intended loop: sample a language from known but interacting computational primitives; train a tensor-transformer; use the known generator to test whether weight-based interpretability recovers those primitives and their interference; use any discoveries to improve the next language and tool. Its early example includes probabilistic noun-to-verb bigrams and skip-bigrams with signed QK/OV interactions. Adding separate task families or a realistic-looking surface helps only insofar as it strengthens that loop. The [alias curriculum result](ALIAS_CURRICULUM_TRANSFER.md) gives one competent binding-dependent joint-rule checkpoint, but it does not yet recover the broader bigram/skip-gram/induction/bracket vocabulary or validate a sparse canonicalization method.

The [original project proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) makes the controlled **joint** data-generating process (DGP) the central asset: specify bigram statistics, skip rules, induction, nesting and their interference, train a tensor-transformer on the resulting language, then use known ground truth to study suppression, error correction, compositional circuits and interpretability tools. The desired loop is to turn any new discovered computation into a new generator rule and repeat.

## What current benchmarks do and do not test

| Benchmark | Ground-truth mechanism | Main use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Original `generator.py` plus `mixed_rule_oracle.py` | Competing bigram/trigram/skip/induction and brackets | Primary interference and circuit-recovery test | 26 artificial tokens; not natural-text evidence |
| `surface_mixture_language.py` | Four competing verb proposals in readable event sentences | Check whether interference results survive a more linguistic surface | Rigid frames, 141 tokens, no realistic lexical diversity |
| `multi_genre_language.py` | Reports, lists, FAQ copying/recall, conditional policies | Transfer across visibly distinct dependency types | Genre header largely separates tasks; weak cross-task interference; 173 tokens |
| FineWeb panel | Observed web text, unknown latent rule labels | Calibrate surface and plausible dependency prevalence | Cannot itself verify synthetic latent mechanisms |

The new multi-genre audit replayed every prefix of 4,000 72-token streams and checked oracle normalization, sampled-token support, list numbering, FAQ object copying and place recall, and policy syntax. The tests passed. It found 6,835 report mixed-verb slots, 4,100 policy mixed-action slots, 3,316 FAQ answer-copy slots, 1,091 FAQ place-recall slots and 4,704 list-number slots. These counts establish coverage, not model mastery.

The realism test is negative. On **480 windows of 72 tokens each**, synthetic text has 157 distinct alphabetic words, mean sentence length 10.5 words and p90 16; the first 72 GPT-2 tokens of each frozen FineWeb window have 6,305 distinct words, mean 16.3 and p90 29. The synthetic and GPT-2 tokenizers differ, so this is still an approximate form comparison, but the mean words per window are close (58.5 versus 54.3). The diversity and sentence-structure gaps rule out a FineWeb-like claim. The added genres are worth retaining as controlled transfer tasks, but broadening the headers does not solve the original DGP problem by itself.

## Next experiment that bears on the thesis

Keep the original mixed-rule and readable report streams as the primary **collision** benchmarks. Train a model on a joint stream where the relative strength of a bigram and induction rule is specified in the visible context. Hold out entire collision patterns or lexical combinations, while maintaining a matched marginal-token control. On fixed prefixes, change only the rule-strength cue and measure the oracle and model shift in next-token log odds. This gives a causal target that ordinary reconstruction or next-token accuracy cannot provide.

Compare raw attention coordinates, a weight-only bond gauge, activation PCA, sparse/intervention-aware bases and a shared-dictionary featurizer at matched dimension and baseline-model fidelity. Evaluate four separate quantities: next-token oracle KL, fidelity of the rule-strength *effect*, selective mediation through the proposed component, and transfer to held-out conflict patterns. A low-rank bond that preserves logits but cannot explain the controlled shift is compression, not recovery of the circuit. Conversely, a component that follows the shift only on seen lexical items is not a reusable rule.

The multi-genre stream should then test whether any recovered component or learning-dynamics claim transfers to FAQ recall, list progression and policy action conflicts. If it does not, identify which dependency changes the circuit. Expand those mechanisms into the joint DGP only when their interactions and observable-prefix oracle are specified; avoid treating separate labeled prompt families as a substitute for interference.

Finally, use FineWeb to estimate the frequency and forms of *actual* dependencies rather than surface cue frequencies alone. Sample matched natural-text spans, manually annotate whether they exhibit copying, induction, list continuation, conditional action, nested closure, etc., and use that to choose mixture rates and distractors. The synthetic oracle remains the ground truth for mechanism tests; the natural-text audit constrains which toy regimes are worth studying.

The proposal's weight-only tensor-circuit ambition remains a hypothesis to test. Exact gauge transformations and useful SVD compression have been shown locally, but neither proves unique functional canonicalization or human-legible components. The success criterion is a compact description that predicts causal behavior on held-out combinations, ideally from weights alone, with data-aware methods as measured comparators.

The proposal's example of a *negative QK effect multiplied by a negative OV effect* is a useful warning about reading a single factor in isolation. For the current models, the recoverable target should be a **signed, composed input-to-output effect** of a known rule, verified by a controlled prefix edit and selective ablation. A weight-only decomposition earns an interpretation claim only when its component predicts those effects beyond the examples used to name it. We should also test whether the same rule appears in different heads or layers across training seeds, rather than assuming the original factor location is canonical.

The later [v5 joint-stream result](SURFACE_MIXTURE_V5_RESULTS.md) is a concrete negative control for this standard. An extra exact counting label improves held-out count accuracy but leaves the model almost invariant when the queried person changes; a 16/64-direction bond truncation preserves that behavior. This is compression of a shortcut, not recovery of a counting circuit. Further tuning of that count task has a stopping criterion: resume only with a model that first passes the query-key and historical-subject causal edits. In the meantime, broaden document-form dependencies and return to the original rule-collision benchmark for weight-only versus data-aware component comparisons.

The first two multi-genre three-layer training seeds sharpen this point. Both learn FAQ copying and place recall with similar exact-oracle and edit scores, but final-token bond patches place most of the copying response at the middle attention bond in seed 0 and at the final attention bond in seed 1. This is an empirical warning against treating a location in one trained parameterization as a canonical description of the function. See `MULTI_GENRE_RESULTS.md` for the interventions, held-out objects, and limits.

The subsequent [weight-only singular-mode ablation](MULTI_GENRE_SINGULAR_MODE_CAUSALITY.md) supplies a narrower positive result: in one trained seed, deleting the second singular score term at each first-layer relative lag damages FAQ recall much more than deleting the top term, while sparing the Policy and FAQ-copying effects. The result replicates on two evaluation panels, but its head and mode index do not transfer to the other trained seed and the vectors have no established linguistic reading. This is a reason to select components by composed causal effect, not score energy alone. It does not replace the original proposal's central test: recovering competing, explicitly sampled language rules and their interference from a jointly trained model.

The [weight-only tag-contrast result](WEIGHT_ONLY_TAG_CONTRAST.md) brings that test back to the original joint generator. Enumerating its 26 tokens and 63 ordinary positions gives an exact first-layer contrast formula from weights alone. Four derived directions mediate almost all of that bond's bigram-versus-induction log-odds response to either rule-mixture tag change, matching a contrast basis fitted on separate no-conflict examples. This meets a *local, intervention-specified* version of the proposal's weight-only ambition; the tag tells the analyst which contrast to study, and the result does not yet recover an unprompted rule or the full model computation.

The [uncued edit prior](ENDOGENOUS_EDIT_WEIGHT_PRIOR.md) is a less favorable but more representative check. On held-out sentence frames, a verb-output weight basis improves later-bond induction mediation over a generic SVD, but an edit-fitted basis remains better and the same weight prior fails on the previous-place rule. A first-bond patch in the deeper readable model mediates almost none of the endogenous induction effect. Further weight-only work should be judged at a causally relevant bond and against both the complete model effect and the exact DGP effect, with a stopping criterion when even full local patching is ineffective.

The [random FineWeb form pilot](FINEWEB_RANDOM_FORM_PANEL.md) redirected the next DGP revision. A cue-independent 32-window sample spans articles, structured records, instructions, essays, personal writing and fiction; even the richer v8 toy remains colon-heavy and has no parenthetical forms. The [alias follow-up](SURFACE_ALIAS_RESULTS.md) now implements a parenthetical alias reused as a later subject, so alias resolution changes the *same lexical-versus-induction collision* rather than creating an isolated labeled task. Its oracle passes replay and supported-edit checks, and a frozen-rate holdout matches only the coarse parenthesis count. The two-layer bilinear model partially uses the earlier definition but does not preserve the oracle's alias-to-canonical-name equivalence; extra causal supervision with either first-choice or balanced edits makes both verb KL and edit fidelity worse. Longer bilinear and softmax runs, two-definition curricula, and a nearly lexical-only control all fail alias substitution. Their output is systematically closer to an equal mixture of both referents than to the selected referent. This is a concrete shortcut hypothesis to test with definition-order and identity interventions. Decomposition should wait for a model that handles both identity-preserving and referent-changing edits on held-out combinations.

The [minimal keyed-lookup capability control](MINIMAL_ALIAS_LOOKUP_RESULTS.md) separates architecture capacity from the joint-stream failure. On exhaustive disjoint test sets, two-layer attention-only bilinear models average 92.5% accuracy on alias–person pairings absent from training across five seeds, with substantially better causal edit fidelity than matched-shell softmax controls. This supports a same-vocabulary curriculum-transfer experiment into the joint readable stream. It does not justify substituting the minimal answer-only lookup for the original proposal's interfering-rule language, nor interpreting its weights as a recovered language circuit.

The [bond-basis comparison on that competent control](MINIMAL_ALIAS_BOND_BASES.md) finds that an eight-direction local weight-SVD basis preserves held-out lookup and query edits at the final attention bond across five seeds. A composed output-weight baseline preserves them too, so the final-bond rank is largely explained by the eight-answer output space. At the first bond, local weight SVD is stronger than the output-weight baseline, but remains a fidelity result. The richer alias stream still fails causal binding even under verb-only oracle supervision; further alias-only loss tuning has a stopping point. The next work should either transfer a keyed circuit with the **same vocabulary and model shape** into the joint DGP or return to other competent joint tasks, and any decomposition must be judged by selective held-out causal effects rather than compression alone.

**Update after that transfer experiment:** [Same-vocabulary pretraining](ALIAS_CURRICULUM_TRANSFER.md) yields pair-restricted joint-language checkpoints that follow previously unseen alias–person key switches in two training seeds. Matched pair-restricted scratch models also learn some key switching after 6,000 updates and obtain comparable or lower average verb KL, but their held-out-pair causal effect errors and identity-preserving edit violations are larger in both seeds. The contrast is evidence that aggregate prediction and causal generalization are separate measurements, not proof that the curriculum is necessary or that either model's bond basis is interpretable. The earlier unrestricted scratch comparison is confounded by the restriction; future claims need more training seeds and a broader rule-transfer check.

The [joint-rule basis transfer check](JOINT_RULE_BASIS_TRANSFER.md) now uses the already trained six-frame model to distinguish pooling known edit responses from transfer to a rule excluded from featurizer fitting. An eight- or sixteen-dimensional basis trained on all three rule edits can mediate induction and previous-place effects across disjoint sentence frames; the basis trained on the *other* rules transfers poorly to the omitted one. This is a negative result for a universal small local response subspace, and a design target for overlapping groups over a shared dictionary. The model itself saw every rule during language training, so the held-out-family claim applies to featurizer fitting only.

The first [shared-group featurizer test](SHARED_GROUP_FEATURIZER_TOY_LANGUAGE.md) puts that design target under a causal criterion. Fitting overlapping groups on native bond activations from lexical/induction contexts does not beat scalar Top-8 on held-out previous-place reconstruction or local mediation across two frame splits. Explicit atom-coverage pressure fixes a learned-membership collapse without fixing the functional gap. A matched paired activation-change objective improves scalar Top-8 more than groups; stronger weighting worsens the grouped result. Keep this negative result and move to downstream causal objectives or more suitable component sites. Repeated reconstruction/change-loss tuning on the same panel would not advance the original proposal's rule-discovery aim.

The [dependency-proxy audit](DEPENDENCY_PROXY_REALISM_AND_REPEAT_TRADEOFF.md) further separates a realistic-looking stream from an informative rule-recovery benchmark. Its raw successor-copy statistic was dominated by fixed phrase continuations; a lexical-successor-conditioned statistic does improve when explicit subject repetition is lowered. That change also removes many exact induction-active verb sites and degrades induction causal fidelity. Reusing non-immediate earlier subjects restores most induction sites while lengthening the dependency. The matched bilinear model learns some longer-range causal response but loses short-range response, a difference that aggregate induction error conceals. Future DGP revisions should diversify sentence scaffolds and content, then evaluate both natural-rate and task-rich regimes. A low rule rate should not be reported as a mechanism-discovery success merely because aggregate language statistics move closer to FineWeb.

The [distance-transfer featurizer comparison](INDUCTION_DISTANCE_FEATURIZER_TRANSFER.md) brings that DGP change back into the decomposition loop. On two test streams and both held-out-frame directions, eight directions fitted on short induction edits inside a 32-dimensional weight prior retain long-distance *model* effects better than generic weight/activation bases; the full local patch gate passes. The model's effect remains much smaller than the exact oracle beyond 48 tokens, and no rule family was withheld from language-model training. This justifies the two-stage local method as a candidate, but redirects the next experiment toward competent, more varied tasks and whole-family featurizer holdouts rather than further sparse rotations of this partial induction response.
