# Sparse featurizers at the earlier section attention bonds

The [attention path screen](V8_SECTION_ATTENTION_PATHS.md) found that the answer reads fact colors mainly in layer 1 of balanced v8 teacher seed 1, but mainly in layer 0 of seed 4. This experiment asks whether the pre-output attention signal at that teacher-specific layer can be represented by scalar sparse coding, disjoint groups, or overlapping groups from a shared dictionary. It extends the [final-bond benchmark](V8_SECTION_BOND_FEATURIZER_RESULTS.md) to a causally earlier site.

The same [whole-association split](results/v8_section_combo_holdout_seed81001.json) is used throughout. Featurizer fit/development contain 240/80 section-key edits and exclude eight section-ID × color associations from *every fact* in their prefixes. Seen and queried-heldout test panels each contain 120 key edits, 80 relevant-color edits, and 80 irrelevant-color edits. The frozen language models were trained on all associations; this is a featurizer-transfer holdout, not a new-task test for the language model.

The [runner](train_v8_section_combo_featurizers.py) captures the answer-position attention bond `z` and the complete residual sequence immediately after its block. It replaces only that answer-position bond by adding `W_O(z_reconstructed - z_native)` to the captured residual, then runs all later blocks and the final readout. Both selected blocks have no MLP. The unmodified bond exactly replays native answer probabilities across all panels (maximum absolute error below `6.0e-7` on GPU, also below `5.7e-7` in the independent CPU baseline runs). Right-padding prefixes is checked against unpadded answers. This is an exact **local intervention**, not a reduction of every attention position in the model.

All learned candidates use fit-endpoint PCA initialization and development-only checkpoint selection. Reconstruction-only scalar Top-8/10/16 models compete with a fixed disjoint 16-atom group model and learned overlapping groups with about 7–9 *actual union-active* atoms. Group and scalar causal variants add loss on the teacher's full answer-probability change from section-key edits. The principal comparison uses 1,200 steps, two independently trained teachers, and two featurizer initializations each. A 3,000-step group-only continuation checks optimization; its checkpoint is selected by the same development rule. No relevant- or irrelevant-color edits are used to fit or select the featurizers.

The table reports **queried-heldout section-key edits**. KL is teacher-to-reconstructed-model KL averaged over original and edited endpoints. Effect error is L1 distance between reconstructed and teacher probability *changes*. Lower is better; A/B are independent featurizer seeds. These are distinct from bond reconstruction error, exact answer accuracy, and cross-entropy, all recorded in the JSON.

Scalar Top-16 is a useful fidelity ceiling, with heldout KL at most `.00005` and key-edit effect error `.0053` in seed 1, and KL about `.00002` and effect error `.0021` in seed 4. The groups have worse bond reconstruction as well as worse functional fidelity at 1,200 steps. After 3,000 steps, the best development-selected shared causal candidate improves to KL `.00858`, effect error `.0457`, 7.4 active atoms for seed 1, and KL `.00499`, effect error `.0415`, 7.1 atoms for seed 4. It still trails scalar Top-8 at similar actual support by roughly 4–9× in KL and 2× in effect error. The 3,000-step shared reconstruction candidates have effect errors `.0641` and `.0546`; disjoint 16-atom candidates remain worse than scalar Top-8 despite twice the support. The improvement with additional training means the 1,200-step group gap had an optimization component. The remaining gap is a result for this architecture, initialization, and objective, not a proof that reusable groups do not exist.

There is also a useful **weight-only** control. A separate corrected run takes right singular directions of the *selected layer's* `W_O`, then keeps the largest 8, 10, or 16 coordinates per endpoint. At rank 16, heldout key-edit KL/effect error is `.00114/.0201` for seed 1 and `.00122/.0203` for seed 4—good local functional fidelity without fitting on the panel. At rank 8, however, it gives `.00990/.0648` and `.01734/.0953`, clearly worse than learned scalar Top-8. The corresponding rank-10 errors are `.00561/.0487` and `.00591/.0485`. The weight basis is a promising prior at moderate rank, but does not automatically identify the best few directions or make individual directions semantic.

**Baseline correction:** the four 1,200-step result files and two 3,000-step group files contain a `weight_svd16` field computed from the *last* layer's `W_O` rather than the selected earlier layer's. Their learned-featurizer results, physical-axis control, native replay, and checkpoints are unaffected. Use only the separate [seed-1 corrected weight controls](results/v8_section_early_bond_seed1_layer1_bond_weight_baselines_corrected.json) and [seed-4 corrected weight controls](results/v8_section_early_bond_seed4_layer0_bond_weight_baselines_corrected.json) for weight-SVD claims. The runner is fixed for subsequent runs.

This establishes a compact *local* functional representation at two teacher-specific attention sites. It does not establish that those 8–16 coordinates form human-legible manifolds, recover a unique computation, preserve the identified answer-to-color **edge** specifically, or compress the full model. The next high-information test should give the groups direct supervision on the measured answer-to-color edge contribution and compare them to scalar coding at matched in-fit reconstruction and teacher KL. If a group still cannot match in-fit fidelity, stop that variant and use the scalar/weight bases to probe directional semantics and transfer across other toy-language rules. An independent unlabeled rule-family holdout is needed before claiming new-task discovery.
