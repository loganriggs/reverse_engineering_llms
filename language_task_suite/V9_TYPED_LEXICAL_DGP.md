# V9 typed lexical realization: broader vocabulary, same form bottleneck

The research target is a variable, language-like **sampled** DGP with known rules, on which weight-based interpretability methods can be compared across generator settings. V9 isolates a lexical setting while keeping V8's world, event order, source attribution, recaps, and clause forms. It is a controlled DGP axis, not the final realistic distribution.

Each document samples consistent names, object nouns, color words, and location words from typed pools. The people/object/indoor-location draws use the frozen V17 corpus-derived frequency counts, with an exponent of zero or one. Location prepositions and color articles remain grammatically compatible. The checker receives **only the visible prefix**: it assigns temporary canonical symbols to observed words in order of appearance, runs the independent V8 answer parser, and maps the result back to the observed word. The temporary symbols need not match the hidden world's canonical names. Rule labels and the sampled lexicon are not passed to that checker.

Two 500-document panels, with frequency exponents zero and one and half the source questions delayed, pass all **4,659** visible-prefix answer sites and matched upstream edits. The syntax-zero re-rendering of each world also passes, with the same answer sequence. The audit covers alias identity, source attribution, owner, color, and place. This verifies sampled-rule integrity; it is not a full next-token marginal oracle. [Semantic audit](results/v9_typed_lexicon_semantic_audit_500x2.json).

V9 roughly doubles word types, but the recurring frames still dominate. Independent FineWeb panels at offsets 38000/39000 have 2,358/2,435 types, top-ten share .207/.217, and first-40 repetition .160/.189. The prior exponent measurably changes **content selection**: over 2,000 sampled lexicons, the ten most often selected person words account for .115 of person slots at exponent zero and .233 at exponent one; object words move from .134 to .276. The aggregate top-word and repetition metrics barely move because they are dominated by structure words. [Lexical-prior audit](results/v9_typed_lexicon_prior_n2000.json); [reference-prevalence form screen](results/v9_typed_lexicon_paired_reference_n64x2.json); [task-rich screen](results/v9_typed_lexicon_paired_source50_n64x2.json).

One actual sample begins: “The violet backpack in the market belonged to Daniel and carried the label Summit. The violet desk on the floor belonged to Rachel and carried the label Pine. A short note about the desk circulated after the sale.” The new vocabulary makes documents less uniform in content, but the same small set of report, update, and recap frames remains obvious. A few incidental clauses still have awkward semantics because they are not generated from an explicit proposition. The lexical substitution protects fixed words such as “a short note” from being mistaken for object mentions, and the visible-only checker is sensitive to type collisions; the frozen pools and semantic audit guard those cases. This is not evidence that the distribution is naturalistic or that a model learned these rules.

V9 is useful as a lexical-frequency stress-test axis for later model and weight-method comparisons, but another model trained solely on V9 would not answer the user's main realism question. The next generator version should jointly sample **discourse acts and their underlying propositions**—for example, introducing a fact, citing a source, correcting a claim, comparing states, asking for a recap, or quoting a report—then realize those acts in multiple syntactic forms. Lexical breadth and discourse structure should be ablated separately and together. Preserve a visible-only answer audit, supported counterfactual edits, whole-rule/composition holdouts, and behavioral eligibility gates before interpreting weights.
