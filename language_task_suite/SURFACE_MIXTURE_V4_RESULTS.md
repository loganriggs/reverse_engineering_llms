# Joint narrative and question retrieval (v4)

`surface_mixture_v4.py` extends the v3 **joint verb and object mixtures** with a third rule family: object-keyed retrieval of a place from narrative history. It also permits ordinary line breaks and occasional numbered lines. The visible prefix determines every next-token distribution; there is no sampled hidden answer. This version is a controlled task suite, **not** a realistic model of FineWeb.

Example from the fixed audit seed:

> In the station Iris keeps the fresh ticket after the concert. Where did the ticket appear? Answer: station. During the season Iris takes the damaged ticket near the park.

If a later question asks about the ticket, the answer is **park**: the rule uses its most recent narrative mention. Questions do not enter the verb/object-rule history. The underlying v3 sentence frames and both mixture weights are unchanged, except that v4 restricts `In the ...` places to plausible interior locations.

The auditor replayed all **216,000** generated prefixes across 3,000 windows of 72 tokens. It independently checked the question answer against the last matching narrative sentence, verified that sampled tokens had positive exact-oracle probability, and checked complete sentence grammar. There were **11,595 verb**, **11,100 object**, and **420 question-answer** slots. Of the answer slots, **78** queried an object from an earlier narrative sentence rather than the latest one, **22** reached at least two narrative sentences back, and **36** had at least two previous mentions whose place changed. A shortcut that always returns the latest narrative place answers **343/420 (81.7%)** correctly. Thus the benchmark includes genuine keyed retrieval and latest-mention cases, but aggregate answer accuracy alone is a weak test. See `surface_mixture_v4_audit.json` for reproducible counts and examples.

The form check used the same frozen 480-window FineWeb panel as v3. In 72-token windows, newlines occur in **63.3%** of synthetic versus **69.0%** of natural windows, and question marks in **13.3%** versus **12.7%**. These two marginal matches do **not** establish realistic language. Colons occur in **27.3%** versus **20.4%**, digits in **17.7%** versus **41.5%**, and quotes and parentheses remain absent versus **22.1%** and **20.6%**. In 457 windows matched on their first 40 alphabetic words, repeated-word fractions are **28.0%** synthetic versus **16.6%** natural; repeated non-stopword fractions are **11.7%** versus **8.4%**. The fixed frames and repeated function words remain a serious discrepancy. The panel also uses a different tokenizer, and these presence rates do not estimate natural task prevalence.

The first model test uses a two-layer, four-head, width-64 attention-only bilinear transformer with RMSNorm, ordinary next-token CE plus exact-oracle verb/object/question expected-CE terms of weight 2 each. A matched control omits only the extra answer term. Evaluation is on a separate generator seed. Report question-answer oracle KL separately from verb/object KL, and report answer accuracy where another narrative sentence intervenes. The paired edit panel changes (1) the queried object, (2) the place in the most recent sentence mentioning that object, and (3) as a no-effect control, the place associated with a different object. Each edited full prefix retains positive oracle support. The second edit is also scored on cases with an intervening narrative sentence to expose last-place shortcuts. Good reconstruction or prediction alone is not evidence of a faithful retrieval circuit.

Both matched 3,000-step runs finished. The frozen training evaluation uses seed 8101; a separate 3,000-window question/edit panel uses seed 9101 and contains 418 answer slots, including 65 with an intervening narrative sentence. The extra answer term markedly improves prediction but trades off verb/object fit:

| Training | Verb KL | Object KL | Answer KL (seed 8101) | Answer accuracy (seed 9101) | Accuracy with intervening narrative |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Verb/object soft targets only | .098 | .255 | 2.181 | 44.3% | 1.5% |
| Plus answer soft target, weight 2 | .160 | .343 | 1.123 | 76.3% | 12.3% |
| Latest-narrative-place shortcut | — | — | — | **84.7%** | 1.5% |

The answer-supervised model is **less accurate than the trivial latest-place shortcut** on the full panel. Its success on the hard subset is real relative to that shortcut, but still poor: 8/65 correct and KL **3.412** nats. The corresponding no-extra-answer model gets 1/65 correct.

The causal edits give the stronger diagnosis (mean L1 error between model and exact-oracle probability *changes*; lower is better):

| Edit | Supported pairs | Oracle effect TV | No-extra-answer L1 | Answer-supervised L1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Change queried object | 245 | 1 | 2.070 | **2.012** |
| Change last place for queried object | 396 | 1 | 2.381 | **.934** |
| Same place edit, with intervening narrative | 58 | 1 | 2.117 | **2.148** |
| Change different object's place (no-effect control) | 213 | 0 | .591 | **1.028** |

The answer-supervised model barely responds to changing the **query key** (mean model TV **.012** for an oracle TV of **1**), and it responds strongly to a place edit that should have no effect (mean model TV **.514**). The apparently good .934 error for changing the queried object's place is driven mainly by recent-place copying; on the 58 cases with an intervening narrative it fails. This is not a recovered object-to-place circuit. More answer loss alone can reward the shortcut while damaging the existing verb/object tasks. The rebalancing experiments below test that shortcut before any component is interpreted as a retrieval feature.

For example, in one held-out prefix an instrument was beside the **courthouse**, followed by a tool in the **tower**. Asked where the instrument appeared, the answer-supervised model's top prediction was **tower** (only .288 probability on courthouse). This example illustrates the measured recency error; it is not used as a substitute for the full edit panel.

The balanced-query option `--hard-query-rate 0.9` makes a question select a nonlatest observed object with 90% probability whenever more than one is available. It leaves narrative rules, question syntax, and document-form frequencies unchanged. A fresh 216,000-prefix oracle audit passes: **231/420** answers now have an intervening narrative and the latest-place baseline falls to **195/420 (46.4%)**. This intervention on the *data-generating process* tests whether the shortcut was rewarded by the original query distribution. The matched training results follow.

Those matched 3,000-step models have now finished. The held-out seed-9101 panel contains 418 questions, 224 with an intervening narrative sentence and 49 with at least two. A **second-last-place** shortcut scores **78.7%** overall and **79.5%** on the intervening subset: moving probability away from the latest object created a new positional shortcut.

| Model trained on hard queries | Answer KL (seed 8101) | Answer accuracy (seed 9101) | Intervening accuracy | Two-plus intervening accuracy | Query-object edit L1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Depth 2, width 64 | .831 | 70.8% | 61.6% | 46.9% | **2.006** |
| Depth 3, width 64 | .814 | 71.5% | 64.3% | 51.0% | **2.007** |

Both models outperform the *latest*-place shortcut on hard questions but fall below the second-last-place baseline overall. Their query-object edit errors remain at the **2.0 no-change baseline**: changing the visible object key should move a deterministic one-hot answer to a different place, but the model's answer distribution hardly changes. Changing the last matching place has error **.921/.950** for depths 2/3, yet error rises to **1.339/1.366** when a narrative sentence intervenes. The no-effect edit on a different object's place still induces false responses, with L1 **.869/.764**. Depth helps ordinary prediction slightly, but not the missing key dependence. The model can use historical place positions without binding them to the queried object.

The next task-design correction is sharper than merely asking older questions: for the **same history**, sample at least two different observed object keys with distinct correct places and comparable probability. Require at least two such keys before a question, then train on the ordinary mixture and optionally paired query-key counterfactuals. Compare held-out answer KL, exact query-key edit effects, and nuisance place edits. This creates irreducible conditional entropy unless the model reads the question key; it directly tests whether causal supervision can induce binding. A whole-task featurizer holdout remains necessary afterward. The current v4 checkpoints should not be mined for a claimed retrieval circuit.

The `--counterbalanced-queries --question-rate 0.08` version implements that correction. At a sentence boundary, a question is available only if at least two previously observed objects have **different** latest places; its key is uniform over all observed objects. A new 216,000-prefix audit checks both the standalone v3 mixtures and every question answer, verifies that each question has at least two distinct possible answers, and verifies the uniform key distribution at the query slot. It found **407 answer slots**; 227 ask about an object other than the latest narrative object. Before seeing the query key, the exact oracle has mean answer entropy **.842 nats**, and even the Bayes-optimal predictor that ignores the key can only reach **44.2%** accuracy on those sampled histories. The question-mark window rate is **13.5%**, versus **12.7%** in the frozen FineWeb panel, although the wider lexical and document-form mismatch remains. On an independent seed-9101, 3,000-window panel, the latest- and second-last-place shortcuts score **44.9%** and **42.6%**; neither can solve this conditional task. The matched results below are judged primarily by the query-key and nuisance edits, alongside aggregate answer accuracy.

The matched counterbalanced baselines use width 64 and 3,000 steps. The depth-2 run reaches answer KL **1.550** on seed 8101 and **42.8%** accuracy on the separate seed-9101 panel (423 questions); depth 3 reaches **1.527** and **46.8%**. On that same panel, the exact best query-blind predictor has **44.7%** accuracy and **.835** nats expected answer CE. Model answer CE is **1.570/1.430** nats for depths 2/3. On **423** supported query-key edits, the oracle's mean effect TV is **1** but the depth-3 model's is **.009**; edit L1 error is **2.008/2.007**, essentially the no-change baseline. Both models also react to a different object's place (false-effect L1 **1.023/1.015**). This confirms that uniform query sampling alone did not induce object-keyed retrieval at this capacity/training duration. The depth-2 control completed on its solo rerun after the first parallel process exited with code 139 before checkpointing. The paired-query control below uses original and edited keys sharing the same narrative history and distinct exact answers. The implementation was smoke-trained and all constructed edited prefixes passed full-support/oracle-answer checks.

The counterfactual depth-3 control keeps the same initialization seed, sampled training stream, architecture, verb/object weights, and total extra answer weight **2**. It divides that weight into **1** on the original exact answer and **1** on one alternative supported query key with a different answer. It forwards the edited rows separately and scores only their answer positions. Success requires answer CE below the key-blind prior on held-out histories, a material query-key effect toward the oracle's TV of 1, and small effects from editing another object's place. Lower ordinary loss alone would not establish binding.

The 3,000-step counterfactual run finished. It improves answer KL on the seed-8101 panel to **1.364** from **1.527** for the matched baseline, while verb/object KL worsens modestly (**.161/.293** versus **.149/.276**). Two independent evaluation seeds show a consistent but incomplete gain:

| Held-out seed | Questions | Baseline / paired-query accuracy | Baseline / paired-query answer CE | Query-key model TV (oracle 1) | Query-key effect L1 (no-change 2) | Nuisance-place effect L1 (oracle 0) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 9101 | 423 | 46.8% / **54.6%** | 1.430 / **1.237** | .009 / **.169** | 2.007 / **1.868** | 1.015 / **.899** |
| 9201 | 408 | 44.6% / **48.5%** | 1.472 / **1.355** | .008 / **.179** | 2.007 / **1.852** | 1.022 / **.950** |

This is evidence that paired causal supervision can move the model toward reading the query key, but it is **not** a faithful retrieval mechanism. Mean query-key response remains less than one fifth of the oracle effect, and changing another object's place still causes a large false response. In seed 9101, answer CE **1.237** also remains above the exact query-blind prior entropy **.835** nats. The two panels replicate evaluation across sampled histories, not training seeds. The single-task retrieval sweep stops here: next work should broaden document/task structure and test whether reusable features transfer across the joint verb, object, and query families with whole-family featurizer holdouts. Only a model with substantially better query-key and nuisance-edit fidelity should support a claimed canonical retrieval circuit.

An exploratory first-layer attention-output bond truncation on the original answer-supervised model compared physical, random, activation-PCA, and weight-SVD bases at several widths. Full-width orthogonal rotations reproduced logits to within **3e-4**, validating the local gauge implementation. At **16/64** directions, answer-slot KL from the full model to the truncated model was **.013** for weight-SVD, **.012** for activation-PCA fitted on all tasks, and **.109** for PCA fitted on narrative-only data. The corresponding oracle answer KL values were **.873**, **.877**, and **.954**, versus **.835** for the untruncated model (76 answer slots in the 512-window panel). Thus data-aware PCA needed exposure to the question family to preserve this model's answer behavior at this width; the weight-only basis did similarly without fitting data. This **does not** show either basis recovered object binding: the model itself largely ignores the query key. Truncation preserves an existing computation, including a shortcut. See the two `bond_gauges_layer0_*fit.json` artifacts for all widths and verb/object slots.

The stopping rule for this retrieval-specific sweep has been met: the generator and counterbalanced variants passed exact-oracle audits, matched baselines and paired supervision were trained, and two held-out panels exposed the remaining key-binding failure. The broader project now needs richer document forms and distinct task families, followed by shared-dictionary or block-sparse featurizer comparisons with whole-task holdouts. The present models are useful negative controls and partial-supervision controls, not ground-truth retrieval circuits.
