# Random-window form check for the joint toy language

Earlier FineWeb inspections selected windows by cue regex; they could show whether a cue referred to a real relation but could not reveal the mix of ordinary document forms. This pilot samples **32 windows at random** from the disjoint 192-window `skip7000` development panel, excluding rows used in its previous cue review. One annotator read the first **160 GPT-2 tokens** of each. The [selection and labels](fineweb_random_form_panel.py) are reproducible; the [JSON](fineweb_random_form_panel.json) stores row IDs, short judgments and a source hash, **not source prose**. The 32 windows are still a small, single-annotator development sample, not a FineWeb-wide prevalence estimate or a held-out final test.

The forms were diverse: **8** structured records/lists/menus or minutes, **8** articles/reports, **7** essays, **3** personal/forum passages, **3** instructional passages, and **3** fiction scenes. The most conspicuous *possible* local relation in each excerpt was attributed speech in **8** cases, field-value binding in **4**, conditional consequences in **3**, entity coreference in **3**, numeric fact binding in **3**, and section/list scope in **3**; four had no clean target in the short excerpt. These are qualitative labels, not a claim that a unique next token follows from each relation. Some windows begin mid-document, and multiple relations can coexist even though the audit records only one primary candidate.

For a coarse surface comparison, each window was cropped to at most **100 alphabetic words**. We compared it with equal-count samples from the six-frame v2 generator and the cleaner low-rate v8 generator. The tokenizers differ and 32 windows give noisy frequencies, so the table identifies conspicuous gaps rather than calibrating exact probabilities.

| Windows containing | Random FineWeb | v2 six-frame | v8 low-rate | Alias stream, low-rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Newline | 29/32 | 0/32 | 29/32 | 0/32 |
| Question mark | 10/32 | 0/32 | 5/32 | 0/32 |
| Colon | 7/32 | 0/32 | 21/32 | 0/32 |
| Quotation mark | 8/32 | 0/32 | 8/32 | 0/32 |
| Parenthesis | 11/32 | 0/32 | 0/32 | 12/32 |
| Digit | 14/32 | 0/32 | 18/32 | 0/32 |

V8 improves document layout and quote exposure, but its colon-heavy headers/questions still stand out and it omits parenthetical structure entirely. The new [alias-in-parenthesis generator](surface_alias_mixture.py), with lower parenthetical and definition-start rates than its task-rich training defaults, puts parentheses in 12/32 sampled windows versus 11/32 natural windows. The **task-rich** setting puts them in 24/32. The lower rates were selected after looking at this development panel, so the near match is **not held-out validation**. This coarse one-cue match does not make the stream realistic: it still has no line breaks, questions, quotations, colons or digits in these windows. V2 remains a useful **controlled interference** benchmark, not a plausible web-text distribution. Natural passages vary in topic, register, speaker, field structure, and syntax far beyond either toy. The reviewed examples often embed the relation in an article or list instead of ending with an explicit `Answer:` prompt.

## Next generator experiment

The first [alias-in-parenthesis implementation](surface_alias_mixture.py) adds this dependency to the *joint* narrative stream rather than another isolated QA genre. A visible definition of a person's alternate name lets a later sentence use that alias as its subject. At the later verb slot, the alias resolves to the canonical person before the ordinary **70/20/10 lexical distribution and previous-subject induction proposal** compete. This makes alias resolution compositional with existing rules, rather than a cosmetic parenthesis count. Two definition wordings and a non-alias aside are present. The [oracle audit](surface_alias_audit.json) verifies sampled-token support, replay, rule-mixture calculation and changed verb distributions under supported alias-key edits. A separate model-side edit test changes the alias referent, changes the alias key, and checks an alias-to-canonical no-effect edit. The outcome of that model test determines whether decomposition is warranted.

The alias task is a **hypothesis**, not a frequency conclusion from one random alias example. It is motivated jointly by the missing parenthetical form here and the explicit aliases in both earlier cue-selected audits. Keep a task-rich training rate and a separate lower-rate realism evaluation. Before interpreting any sparse or weight-only component, require a model to beat a referent-blind baseline on whole-alias and whole-wording holdouts, preserve ordinary lexical/induction fidelity, and respond selectively to referent edits. If it fails, shorten the binding distance and test a standard-attention control before further decomposition.

The random development sample improves the **design evidence** but does not verify that the new generator matches natural language or that a bilinear model learns its new rule. The `skip11000` panel's semantic contents were reserved while the grammar and rates were chosen.

After fixing the low-rate parameters at parenthetical/definition-start/alias-reuse **.25/.10/.10**, a [coarse held-out form check](alias_surface_holdout.json) decoded the reserved `skip11000` panel **only for counts**, without inspecting its prose or semantic relations. It has zero exact token-row overlap with `skip7000`. At 100-word crops, parentheses occur in **63/192** natural windows and **61/192** alias-stream windows. All the other measured form markers remain far apart: natural windows have **180** newlines, **30** questions, **57** colons, **56** quotation marks and **128** digit-containing excerpts, versus **zero** for each in the alias stream. The parenthesis-rate match generalizes across these panels; the toy as a whole plainly does not match web text. No further rate tuning should use this held-out panel.
