# Multi-genre v3: agreement across an intervening noun

This version adds a subject–verb agreement dependency to the existing Reports, Guide, FAQ, Policy, and Catalog streams. It is motivated by targeted agreement-attractor and relative-clause evaluations ([Linzen et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01368); [Marvin and Linzen 2018](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1151/)). The generator is synthetic: the genre header, eight noun pairs, fixed frames, and short sentence lengths do **not** establish similarity to FineWeb. Its purpose is an exact, auditable rule with both positive and no-effect causal edits.

Examples of the generator's support:

- `Agreement: The pilots near the doctor are ready.` The main subject `pilots`, not the nearest noun `doctor`, determines `are`.
- `Agreement: The pilots that the doctor helps are ready.` The embedded `helps` agrees with `doctor`; the later `are` agrees with `pilots`.

The subject and intervening noun numbers are sampled independently, so agreement and attraction-conflict cases are equally likely in expectation. The visible-prefix oracle returns the next-token distribution and a rule label. The six genres share one vocabulary, and `train_multi_genre.py --language v3` can train on any genre mixture. Catalog remains a separate known-failing keyed-retrieval task and is assigned zero weight in the mixed pilot below.

`audit_multi_genre_v3_agreement.py` replayed every prefix in 100 independent 129-token agreement-only streams and another 100 mixed-genre streams. It checked sampled-token support, distribution normalization, main/embedded verb choices, and grammar-preserving counterfactuals. The agreement-only panel contained 757 matching and 740 conflicting main-verb sites. All 1,497 main-subject flips changed the main verb; all 1,497 attractor-number flips left it unchanged. At 578 embedded-verb sites, flipping the attractor changed that verb. The audit also checked 920 no-effect preposition swaps. These are generator checks, not model results.

An attention-only width-64, four-head bilinear transformer trained on agreement-only streams for 1,500 AdamW steps with auxiliary agreement CE weight 2. Depths 1–3 all reached exact-oracle KL below 0.0001 nats at main match, main conflict, and embedded sites on an independent 128-stream panel. The independent 128-stream valid-edit panel contains 3,444 pairs. At depth 1, effect L1 error is 0.000277 for 1,025 main-subject flips, 0.000036 for 368 embedded-attractor flips, and at most 0.000462 across four no-effect edit families. Depths 2–3 are similarly accurate. This is evidence of output-level rule competence on fresh combinations from the same finite grammar. It does not establish a unique internal circuit, broad syntactic generalization, or transfer to natural text.

In a six-genre stream with Catalog weight zero and agreement weight 0.17, 2,500-step depth-2 and depth-3 models without RMSNorm retain near-zero agreement KL and edit error. On 751 fresh valid agreement edit pairs, every effect L1 error is below 0.0002. They do **not** pass the older FAQ place-recall gate: KL is 3.196/2.830 nats. Report verb mixture KL is 0.373/0.368, policy action mixture 0.481/0.485, and FAQ copying 0.006/0.002. A three-layer RMSNorm run with the same mixture reaches FAQ recall KL 1.541 at step 2,000 but worsens to 3.237/3.239 at steps 2,500/3,000; its final agreement KL remains near zero, with edit errors below 0.0005. The earlier four-genre benchmark used RMSNorm, 3,000 steps, and a different genre mix; these runs do not isolate the effect of adding agreement. The mixed task is currently not a competent joint teacher for FAQ place recall, and the normalized run is unstable at this learning rate/objective.

Next use the agreement task as an additional **labeled causal target** for weight-only versus data-aware canonicalization, sparse paths, and shared-group featurizers. Keep other entire task families out of any featurizer fit, then test their prediction and causal edits independently. For agreement itself, move beyond the finite frame with held-out noun combinations, longer distractors, and additional syntax forms before claiming a general linguistic feature. The present task's negative controls are especially useful: a representation that changes the main verb when only the nearby noun changes has not recovered the rule.
