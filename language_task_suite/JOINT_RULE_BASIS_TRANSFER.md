# Can one local basis mediate several joint-language rules?

The [original proposal](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GncmyuKmp526RJpmj/ambitious-mech-interp-w-tensor-transformers-on-toy-languages) asks whether interpretability methods recover known, interfering language computations. The [previous uncued-edit result](ENDOGENOUS_EDIT_WEIGHT_PRIOR.md) tested a weight-only output prior separately on induction, previous-place and lexical edits. This follow-up asks whether a *single* intervention-trained local subspace can cover those rules, and whether directions learned from two rules transfer to a **held-out third rule family**. The language model was trained on all rules and all sentence frames; only the basis-fit data have a family holdout.

The source is the two-layer width-64 bilinear checkpoint in [surface_mixture_v2_soft_models](surface_mixture_v2_soft_models/results.json). Its exact-prefix DGP mixes a subject-dependent lexical proposal with previous-verb induction, lead cue and previous-place proposals at the same verb prediction. Valid paired edits change one proposal while keeping the prefix supported. We use the **final attention bond**, where a complete last-position patch mediates most of the model's induction and previous-place effects. A lexical edit also travels through other paths: the complete local patch differs from the complete model effect by L1 **1.100/.977** on forward/reverse frame splits. Lexical rows below therefore evaluate only local-bond fidelity, not the full lexical computation.

For each fit split, we form equal-sized samples of unit-normalized attention-bond response vectors from the relevant rule-edit families and take their leading SVD directions. `pooled` includes lexical, induction and previous-place edits; `leave out` excludes the tested rule and fits only on the other two. `own` fits only the tested rule. The fit frames (`lead/subject/at`) and test frames (`when/in-place/during`) come from separate generator seeds; the reverse split swaps their roles. A nuisance-adjective edit is an oracle-stable selectivity control. Every basis is evaluated at the **same width** with the same test edits. Numbers are mean L1 difference between the restricted patch's probability effect and a complete 64-direction patch at this bond; lower is better.

| Test rule | Width | Own edit basis, forward/reverse | Pooled basis, forward/reverse | Leave rule out, forward/reverse | Weight SVD, forward/reverse |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Induction | 8 | .124/.118 | .164/.176 | .202/.229 | .230/.256 |
| Previous place | 8 | .139/.151 | .202/.208 | .481/.486 | .406/.399 |
| Lexical cue | 8 | .420/.513 | .491/.539 | .646/.584 | .633/.671 |
| Induction | 16 | .055/.061 | .052/.062 | .106/.106 | .089/.106 |
| Previous place | 16 | .039/.043 | .084/.101 | .238/.244 | .137/.156 |
| Lexical cue | 16 | .156/.282 | .143/.241 | .467/.420 | .325/.300 |
| Induction | 32 | .028/.026 | .020/.020 | .046/.043 | .007/.007 |
| Previous place | 32 | .024/.028 | .028/.029 | .113/.086 | .013/.013 |
| Lexical cue | 32 | .044/.068 | .049/.093 | .299/.278 | .011/.012 |

At width 8, the pooled basis covers induction and previous-place less well than each matched basis but far better than the leave-out basis for previous place. At width 16, pooling is close to matched on induction and lexical edits, while previous-place remains less well represented. The leave-out basis performs worse than generic weight SVD on previous-place at all three widths. Thus the fitted directions do **not** transfer as a generic new-rule representation; family-specific response directions remain important. Width 32 weight SVD is already near the full local patch, so its high fidelity is a broad-compression control, not evidence of a discovered rule. On nuisance-adjective edits, the full-patch TV is only **.0055/.0085**, and the width-8 pooled relevant basis induces TV **.0043/.0061**; the experiment does not show a large nuisance response.

This is one trained model seed, two independent edit/frame panels, and a local attention-bond test. It does not identify the heads or the computational route, prove a unique canonical basis, or demonstrate **new-task discovery**: all rule families were named before fitting. It suggests a concrete shared-dictionary featurizer test: learn reusable directions with distinct sparse rule gates, fit on two rule families, and require an unlabeled group to emerge for the held-out third family. Compare reconstruction, next-token oracle KL, full-model causal-effect fidelity, local selective mediation and genuine held-out-family discovery separately. A pooled subspace that merely compresses all three known edits is a useful baseline for that test.
