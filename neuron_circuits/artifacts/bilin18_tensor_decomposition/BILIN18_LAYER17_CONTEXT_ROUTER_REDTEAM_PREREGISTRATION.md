# Preregistration: Layer-17 context-router red team

The context-operator atlas found that a discovery-frozen current-token lookup
reproduces 0.81--0.89 of the rank-16 local operator on three corpora. This may
reflect reusable lexical routing, but it may also be produced by the strong
mean operator, token class, absolute position, repeated token identities, or
train/test vocabulary overlap.

Before inspecting any of the tests below, this follow-up freezes the same
tensor core, discovery/evaluation ranges, and position panel. It compares:

- the mean, position, coarse-token, coarse-token-plus-position, exact-token,
  and exact-token-plus-position routers;
- a ridge map from the model's native token embeddings to context coordinates;
- 64 permutations of token identities matched within coarse class and
  log-frequency bin;
- count-conditioned performance on tokens observed 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16 times;
- and early-position-trained routers tested late, plus the reverse direction.

All router predictions are scored in the tensor-induced operator metric. The
matched permutations preserve lookup coverage, token-class composition,
training frequency, the table's coordinate vectors, and the operator tensor.
They destroy only the pairing between a lexical identity and its learned
context-coordinate mean.

Success would identify lexical identity or embedding geometry as a reusable
but lossy controller of the local operator. Failure would retain the compact
operator family while rejecting the stronger claim that its routing is
lexically organized. Neither outcome makes individual context axes canonical.
