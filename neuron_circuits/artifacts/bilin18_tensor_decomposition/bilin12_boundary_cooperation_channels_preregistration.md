# Preregistration: cooperative bilinear terms and native-channel frontier

The first analytic router assay showed that cross and edit-squared terms are each
causally poor while their exact sum is strong.  Its shuffled-base control evaluated
the already-insufficient cross term alone, so it did not test whether correct
base/edit pairing matters to the complete computation.

This correction combines the norm-matched shuffled-base cross term with the intact
quadratic term before intervention.  It also measures projected write cosine and
the norm cancellation ratio between the two analytic components.

Using the same frozen rank-32 boundary-write basis, rank native MLP channels on the
FineWeb discovery panel by mean absolute activation change times the norm of their
projected down-write.  Freeze top-k sets for k in 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 and
compare them with fixed random sets.  A per-example oracle top-k based on the same
contribution score measures the possible gain from contextual channel routing.

This separates three organizations that share the same compact output subspace:
a stable neuron module, a context-selected native module, or broad cooperative
bilinear cancellation.
