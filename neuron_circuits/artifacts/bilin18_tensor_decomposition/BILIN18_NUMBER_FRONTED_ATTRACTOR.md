## H587 — Subject dominance persists when the grammatical subject is the second noun

H586 left the subject/first-noun confound unresolved. Freeze the same H584 direction and reuse H586's crossed subject/attractor numbers and regular/irregular lexical pairs in a fronted-preposition frame: “Beside the guards, the child usually”. The attractor is now first and grammatical subject second. There are 64 native prompts and 1,408 interventions, with the same full/zero, ranks1/4/8 and matched nuisance/random controls. No basis refit or scale tuning.

All four type/attractor-number competence cells pass. Both-endpoint candidate accuracy is 1.000; full-vocabulary top1 ranges 0.563–0.938. The regular plural-attractor cell is only modestly above the gate, and individual errors remain. No item filtering. These are the same 16 lexical pairs as H586 in a new frame, not independent populations.

All 32 subject singular-to-plural coefficient contrasts are positive. The subject main effect remains larger than the attractor effect, although by only about two to three times here, compared with roughly four to nine times across H586 frames. Attractor contrasts are also positive. The code is mixed, not a perfectly isolated grammatical variable.

Together with H586, this weakens a fixed-first-noun-only account (subject remains dominant when second), a nearest-noun-only account (subject was dominant when the attractor was nearer), and a regular-suffix-only account (irregular plurals transfer). It remains compatible with context-dependent role weighting, template-specific routing, or other syntactic/lexical heuristics. These finite templates do not prove general grammatical abstraction.

The fronted frame has a much smaller causal effect through this writer. Full subject donor movement is 0.392 regular and 0.444 irregular, compared with 2.373/2.278 in the unfronted prepositional frame. Rank-one projection gives 0.305/0.345, but its relative margin error is 0.364/0.314 and relative reference KL 0.582/0.494. Rank eight does not restore the original fidelity. Retaining much of the signed mean effect is therefore not equivalent to reproducing the whole intervention.

Both the written coefficient and the downstream sensitivity may have changed. Dividing aggregate effects by aggregate coefficients is only suggestive because nonlinear responses and item-level variation matter. Next inject identical signed amplitudes along the frozen direction across the three frames, crossed with subject/attractor number and lexical type. Set dose sizes from the original discovery write scale, not these outcomes. Measure finite-difference agreement response, KL and answer-group loss; compare against norm-matched random/nuisance directions. This separates variation in what the writer encodes from variation in how downstream computation reads the same residual direction. It is a direct test of context-dependent computational use of a shared representation.
