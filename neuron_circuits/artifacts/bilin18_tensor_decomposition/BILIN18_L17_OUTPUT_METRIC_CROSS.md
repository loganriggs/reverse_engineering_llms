With common response lengths, changing destination from ordinary states to the tail raises mean exact KL 13.16x for tail-origin responses and 33.47x for ordinary-origin responses. At the tail destination, ordinary-origin directions produce 1.70x the KL of tail-origin directions. Thus the large selected-case effect is not confined to special response directions produced at the tail. The receiving output state is broadly sensitive over this tested response set. There is still origin-by-destination dependence; no unique additive mediation claim is warranted.

For original-length matched tail responses, the main signed token contribution is “ian”, a plausible continuation after the context ending “Kerry Kill”. Its baseline probability is0.404635 and its mean edited probability0.458079. Its mean signed KL contribution0.069867 exceeds total KL0.057616 because other token contributions can be negative. It must not be reported as a percentage of KL explained. Token identity locates an effect but does not establish that synthetic source vectors or selected MLP factors encode that name.

An exact decomposition across the48 source interventions separates mean individual KL 0.057616 into KL of the mean prediction versus baseline 0.009020 plus dispersion around that mean 0.048595. Dispersion supplies 84.34% of this fixed-set mean KL. “ian” probabilities range from0.2574 to0.6533, so the interventions do not all produce one coherent direction of change. This weakens an interpretation based merely on the positive average shift. The decomposition is exact for these predictions, not population inference.

Numerical audits: all matched-origin/destination Fisher and KL scores replay H431, maximum relative discrepancy 2.2e-11 (frozen tolerance1e-6). Independent array aggregation reproduces all eight cells. The KL mean/dispersion decomposition closes to 6.94e-18. No scientific sign/equivalence claims are newly confirmed.

Research implication: the behavioral importance of a vector cannot be judged solely by where it was produced or its magnitude. The receiving context’s probability geometry strongly changes its consequences, even among equal-length directions. This supports evaluating proposed units jointly with their context-dependent output effects; it does not prove a universal context-dependent ontology or rule out stable semantic features elsewhere.
