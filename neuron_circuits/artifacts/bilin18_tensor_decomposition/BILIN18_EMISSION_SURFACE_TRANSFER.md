## H623 — Frozen final vector transfers across capital surfaces but reverses on a language construction

The previous turn was progress: H622 localized most of the frozen effect to Attention5's final output position. H623 freezes that1152-dimensional vector and tests three relations × three surface forms × eight items (72 prompts). Capital forms end in "is" or "Answer:"; language forms end in "is", "speak", or "Answer:"; plural forms include declarative and question-answer prompts. Sentence lengths vary. Test native, addition1, subtraction0.25/1, and equal-norm random-complement addition/subtraction1. No coefficient fitting, direction refitting or recipient scaling occurs.

Answer-format scoring is expanded to two explicitly enumerated continuations per candidate: a leading-space direct answer and a leading-space/opening-double-quote answer prefix. The quoted event stops immediately after the answer, without requiring a closing quote. Tokenized prefixes are checked to be mutually disjoint, and their probabilities are summed per identity. This is probability of specified answer beginnings, not general semantic correctness or probability of all possible answer forms. There are432 prompt-condition records supported by1,728 sequence evaluations across288 unique teacher-forced prefix queries per mode. During every continuation forward, the intervention remains at the original prompt's final position; it is not moved to each newly appended token.

**Capital transfer extends beyond the original six-token scaffold.** On short prompts such as "France's capital is", adding the vector raises correct-token top1 from12.5% to87.5% and answer-prefix mass0.0485→0.2483. Subtraction lowers mass to0.0027 and top1 to0%. On capital question-answer prompts, addition raises mass0.1773→0.2045 and top1 from50% to62.5%; subtraction lowers mass to0.0763 and top1 to0%. Every capital identity remains best among the eight scored candidates across all modes. This supports a reusable intervention on these relations, beyond the original length and final token, but does not imply that all factual rankings or probabilities are invariant.

**A language construction reverses the effect.** On "People in France speak" and corresponding country prompts, addition lowers intended-answer-prefix mass0.1705→0.1258 and candidate accuracy75%→62.5%; subtraction raises mass to0.2035. In contrast, language question-answer prompts show addition0.7413→0.7630 and subtraction0.5852, with perfect candidate/top1 accuracy throughout. The same vector is therefore not a universally signed answer-emission control. Relation, wording, final token and downstream state vary together, so this experiment cannot identify which causes the reversal.

Random controls do not reproduce the large short-capital effect: random subtraction changes its mass to0.0643, compared with0.0027 for the common vector. Random effects are not zero everywhere; language form0 random subtraction also changes candidate accuracy, so control results must remain visible rather than being treated as universal nulls.

**Candidate-set accuracy can be badly misleading.** All plural prompts have100% choice accuracy among the eight plural-word candidates, but extremely low answer-prefix mass (native0.0008–0.0048) and0% direct-token top1. Adding an opening-double-quote variant recovers some mass but does not resolve this discrepancy. The set contained only plural forms of different nouns; it omitted plausible errors such as repeating the singular noun. Hence100% here means identifying which candidate noun relates to the prompt, not necessarily computing pluralization.

A descriptive native greedy audit generates eight tokens on all24 plural prompts. None contains the intended plural word. Typical outputs are ' "cat" and the plural form of', ' “dog”.', and 'The plural of book is book.' Other prompts start with single/curly quotes or explanations that were not in the two scored variants. This shows both incomplete format coverage and actual wrong-form continuations in the tested short rollouts. It does not establish global inability: only these prompts and eight greedy tokens were examined. The audit was not used to modify scoring or filter results. Future competence tests should include singular forms and other plausible errors as competitors, not only unrelated correct-type answers.

Quoted prefixes contribute little to capital/language mass and modestly to the already tiny plural mass. We therefore cannot explain the transfer reversal as an artifact of omitted opening-double-quote answers. Other formats remain outside the measured event set. Teacher-forced prefix probability and greedy generation are different measurements; neither alone provides an exhaustive semantic evaluation.

Current hypothesis update: a fixed final-layer5 output direction can modulate factual answer emission over multiple capital formats, but its sign and identity effects depend on recipient context. A context-sensitive readout of a reusable direction is better supported than a universal scalar answer variable. A simpler vocabulary bias, interacting with which answers are currently likely, remains a live alternative. The plural audit warns that apparent task transfer must be distinguished from noun matching under an incomplete candidate set.

Next quantify the small-dose logit response to this same vector across contexts. Compare a common vocabulary-wide response with context-dependent responses, preserving all candidate and generic-token effects. In particular, ask whether language reversal reflects a stable preference for particular language tokens or a change in the downstream transformation of the vector. Freeze any predictive response fit on a declared discovery subset and test other surfaces; report exact answer-versus-plausible-error contrasts. This targets the mechanism of the direction rather than collecting only more successful intervention examples.
