## H940 — Factor normalization can erase the proposed convex-hull prior

Before implementing a constrained version of H939's normalized bilinear factors, audit its actual feasible set. Use two disjoint128-state anchor subsets from the512 training states. Compare raw states, mean-centered states, independently observed random intervention differences, and raw states translated by5 along a nuisance axis. No evaluation examples define anchors. Teacher axes are used only to certify geometry, never to learn a representation.

For each bank solve32 linear programs maximizing radius t on +/-16 orthonormal teacher-basis directions, with nonnegative convex weights summing to1. Positive radii on all signed axes imply that their convex hull contains a neighborhood of0: a ball of radius at least min(t)/sqrt(16). Save the convex-weight witnesses and check equality residuals and primal-dual gaps. This is a precise geometric check, not an A-SAE reproduction or a learned-model comparison.

Both raw and centered banks contain a ball of radius at least .0144–.0150; difference banks at least .0733–.0826. Therefore, if a factor is implemented as normalize(sum_i w_i*anchor_i), EVERY unit direction is feasible for all these banks. A hull parameterization can still change optimization and implicit regularization, but cannot be credited here with restricting representable directions. This depends on origin being interior; translated banks explicitly fail that condition.

Even without normalization, each true unit signal axis has a convex-hull representative at radius .058–.093 in raw banks. Four product outputs recover the exact teacher by compensating with output weights133–260, instead of unit weights. Native and finite-edit teacher outputs replay to1.49e-12 using these unnormalized convex factors. Thus banning explicit factor normalization alone does not prevent functional scale compensation. H939's output-weight penalty would charge this solution, so at a fixed penalty the constraint changes effective regularization; a fair comparison must expose that tradeoff rather than call it geometric identification.

Difference banks permit true signal radii .293–.460, so a fixed output-gain penalty favors them partly through scale. Rescaling anchor banks changes this advantage. A state-versus-difference win would require anchor-scale, output-gain and initialization controls before attributing it to a better computational prior. Constant background translations can also alter feasibility; proper affine state baselines and component directions must be distinguished.

Verification:256 ray LPs,8 origin-feasibility checks, saved witnesses; normalized axis replay6.47e-12, full teacher replay1.49e-12. The shifted-bank projection onto the translation axis is strictly positive for every anchor, independently certifying0 is outside that hull. All source hashes unchanged; no original-model or reserved-test evaluations. An initial origin-only diagnostic was wrong because maximizing t with a zero ray leaves t unconstrained. That run was rejected and preserved in work; fixing t=0 in origin checks and rerunning all cases repairs it. Ray results themselves were unaffected.

Research decision: do not implement normalized-hull factors and describe them as a genuinely narrower direction family. Compare native-scale convex factors with an explicit gain budget against free factors under the same declared budgets; include normalized-hull as a parameterization/optimization control, not a hard directional prior. Vary anchor samples and initialization independently. The central question remains whether a chosen prior improves compact prediction of independent interventions, with supervised known-factor recovery reported honestly. These results do not refute Archetypal SAEs, whose decoder/coding conventions differ from this bilinear student.
