## H632 — Bilinear-context sensitivity extends across sampled directions

H631 was progress: fresh countries reproduced the original sign contrast while wording and competence limited its interpretation. H632 asks whether that contrast is peculiar to the discovered vector or reflects a broader local transformation.

Nine equal-norm orthogonal directions were frozen before evaluation: the original vector, four orthogonalized directions from the existing learned 64-dimensional span, and four seeded Gaussian directions projected outside that span and the other probes. The original vector was historically selected; this is not an unbiased comparison of nine random features. Control signs are arbitrary.

The 36 H631 prompts and exact original four capital reference contexts remain fixed. They include 24 prospective prompts: eight fresh countries crossed with three language constructions. For each direction, analytically recompute the initial block5 tangent, then propagate it through native operators, through bilinear-context replacements at MLP6–9, or through normalization-Jacobian replacements there. Later operators and final readout remain native. These are local derivative counterfactuals, not finite activation replacements or task-accuracy interventions.

**Context sensitivity is not confined to the discovered vector.** In all216 prospective prompt–direction pairs, bilinear-context replacement changes the full-vocabulary response more than normalization replacement does. This counts repeated measurements, not216 statistically independent trials. The metric is the norm of the change in centered logit derivatives, weighted by the model's native token probabilities; it describes local output-distribution sensitivity.

On fresh “People in … speak”, the original vector has mean context-change norm0.553 versus normalization-change norm0.021. The eight controls have context-change norms0.149–0.250 versus normalization-change norms0.011–0.044. Their mean per-example relative context changes range0.782–1.683, compared with2.357 for the original. Thus the original is especially sensitive on this construction, but substantial sensitivity also appears inside and outside the learned span.

Large full-vocabulary response changes also occur in the fully competent main-language cell; intended-token sign reversal is not the only evidence. On the original speak construction, the original vector again changes from negative intended-token derivative in all eight cases to positive in all eight under context replacement. Controls show heterogeneous signs, and reversing a control direction reverses its derivative. Their signs should not be read as inherent semantic labels.

**A compact description is plausible but not yet demonstrated to transfer.** Among the nine sampled directions on fresh “People in … speak”, the leading eigenvalue accounts for67.6% of the context-change Gram-matrix trace; the top two account for75.1%. The corresponding native-response fractions are29.8% and51.7%. Across other fresh constructions, context-change top-two fractions are79.4% and60.5%. These are eigenspectra of prompt-averaged, probability-weighted response Gram matrices. They measure energy concentration within nine input probes only. They do not establish the full operator's rank, a shared output basis across contexts, or a unique canonical form. The particularly large original-vector response can contribute to concentration. No low-rank model was fitted or prospectively tested here.

The most supported interpretation is a context-conditioned transformation affecting multiple directions, with the discovered vector unusually sensitive on the original construction. A single isolated semantic direction is insufficient to describe these measurements. Still viable are a small transferable family of transformations, a larger task-specific transformation, or generic sensitivity to replacing operating contexts. This experiment does not distinguish those explanations decisively.

Next test frozen response models across genuinely different tasks: copying, factual completion, inflection with singular competitors, agreement, and simple numeric or symbolic continuations, retaining a competence audit for each. First capture the native transformations and compare context-conditioned predictions with a context-independent baseline on held-out directions, templates and identities. Use matched within-task reference replacements alongside cross-task replacements to distinguish generic replacement damage from structured context effects. Choose rank or context groups on a discovery split only; evaluate full-vocabulary local fidelity and finite-dose KL separately from intended-token effects and accuracy. Failure should identify whether the missing information is direction, context, or nonlinear dose. This remains a proposed next experiment, not a completed result.
