# Preregistration: causal router-subspace and threshold red team

The depth trace found smoothly increasing predictability and smoothly
increasing causal dependence, with all three corpora first crossing 0.90 mean-
replacement fidelity at the sampled Block-8 boundary. This can be overread as
a discrete compilation phase transition, and full-state replacement does not
show that the fitted predictive directions are themselves causal.

This follow-up adds the missing Blocks 5, 7, and 9, sweeps reporting thresholds
0.95, 0.90, and 0.80, and compares six finite interventions:

- full-state mean replacement and cyclic substitution;
- replacement only inside the 16-dimensional column span of the layer's
  discovery-frozen ridge router; and
- replacement inside a seeded isotropic random 16-dimensional state subspace.

All edits preserve the orthogonal state component, original embedding, and
persistent value bus, then recompute the full remaining suffix. Overlapping
full-state cells are compared with the parent trace as a small-panel replication.

The router span is a supervised predictive object and need not be unique. A
positive causal result supports it as a useful intervention coordinate, not as
the model's uniquely correct feature basis. Threshold crossings are descriptive
summaries of a curve; different thresholds or corpora may imply different
“boundaries” without contradiction.
