## H477 — Learning coefficients in a frozen output space

Follow H476's oracle finding with a learned response predictor. Freeze the rank128 input coordinates and 16-dimensional output basis from H472. Predict 16 coefficients linearly from the change in 128 kernel coordinates; the output dimension, input support, number of learned coefficients (2048), and source family stay fixed. No oracle native response is used at prediction time beyond the native intact baseline shared by all response-isolation experiments.

Train on the original 256 receivers and both signed interventions using the first32 donor vectors cyclically. Compare raw squared-response regression with receiver-dependent baseline-Fisher weighted regression. Both use the actual native training responses. Fisher Gram matrices and target inner products come only from training receivers; normalize by mean Gram diagonal. Solve the full 2048-dimensional regularized normal equations in float64, anchoring the coefficient matrix to the original map. Ridge multipliers are [1e−6,1e−4,.01,1] times the mean Hessian diagonal. These are globally optimal solutions to their regularized quadratic objectives, not finite-KL optima.

Select each method by pooled finite KL on the original 128 validation receivers, including the original predictor as a fallback. Freeze selections before evaluating the original128 test receivers and the already-inspected H473512 receivers. Those panels are disjoint from coefficient fitting but historically reused; neither is pristine confirmation. The output basis itself was selected using the earlier validation history.

Validation selects the original map for raw regression and ridge1 for Fisher regression. Validation mean KL is .000889208 originally and .000856921 for the selected Fisher fit. Near-unregularized Fisher fitting gives .001194716, worse than original; ridge.01 gives .000895962. Thus validation favors a conservative change anchored to the prior predictor. Ridge1 is the largest tested finite penalty: this grid does not establish a globally optimal regularization strength. Raw and Fisher penalty scales normalize their respective Hessians and need not correspond to equal changes in predictions.

No paired confidence intervals or new success gates are claimed; point improvements on reused panels are not fresh evidence of a generalization guarantee.

Interpretation: the fixed output basis has capacity that the learned input-to-coefficient map does not yet access. Changing only the regression metric produces modest transfer. Weakly regularized fitting hurts validation, so simply solving the training quadratic more exactly is insufficient. This does not isolate the cause: training sample coverage, regularization, the fixed input representation, local-versus-finite objective mismatch, and optimization of the original model remain possible explanations. The linear coefficient class is convex for the quadratic objectives tested, eliminating factorized nonconvex optimization as an explanation of their training solution, not of the whole research problem.

Audits: original output-map projection into the saved basis has relative error below1e−6; all regularized normal equations have relative residual below1e−7; all saved per-case arrays finite. Direct coefficient-delta evaluation avoids subtracting rounded full predictions. Aggregate original KL recovery differs from H473 by below1e−5 at both signs; this small numerical drift is measured rather than treated as a scientific gain. Candidate maps and the validation selection are saved before evaluation. No full-state/intercept reconstruction or intact CE check is performed here, so this is a response predictor comparison, not a validated full-layer replacement. All document counts remain13504.
