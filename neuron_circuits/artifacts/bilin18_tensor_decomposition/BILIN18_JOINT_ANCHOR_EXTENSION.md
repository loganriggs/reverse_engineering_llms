KL here is KL(native model output || reconstructed-state output), in nats, not the full-donor-reference KL used in prior experiments. Top1 agreement measures fidelity to native predictions, not task correctness or ground-truth next-token accuracy. Small KL does not establish preserved computation everywhere, especially at a single site.

Mean per-state squared reconstruction error relative to x-minus-old-task-center drops from0.572to0.504 on new eligible states (~11.9%relative reduction), while their output KL rises~1.4%. The extra anchor preserves one additional native top1 among40states, which does not overturn the distributional result. Old-task KL rises~0.27% with unchanged top1. Natural KL falls~0.62%, a small descriptive change with unchanged top1; no statistical or broad-generalization claim. Natural reconstruction ratios are0.617→0.616. These are averages of per-state normalized errors, not H998's ratio of aggregate squared norms, so the figures should not be equated.

The added subspace guarantees non-increasing Euclidean reconstruction error and the audit verifies that invariant for every state. It does not guarantee lower downstream KL. The matched-size random extensions control the extra dimension and do not show an anchor advantage on new-task full-output KL. This is a failed whole-state fidelity improvement despite a successful paired causal contrast.

Interpretation: a direction can preserve donor-recipient changes while its absolute single-state coefficient interacts poorly with errors remaining elsewhere. Task centering/domain shift, missing complementary directions, and downstream nonlinear sensitivity are plausible explanations; none is established by this experiment. Strong paired-edit preservation alone is insufficient as a featurizer objective. Keep the noun direction as a provisional controlled contrast, not a demonstrated improved featurizer or a complete computational unit.
