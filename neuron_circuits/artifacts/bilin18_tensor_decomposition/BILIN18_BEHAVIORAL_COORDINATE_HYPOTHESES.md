## H501 hypothesis refresh — Twenty explanations and discriminating tests

This is a refreshed hypothesis tournament after H498–501, not twenty independent established findings. Support labels are qualitative and scoped to these experiments; no numerical posterior probabilities are implied. Several explanations can hold simultaneously.

The best-supported current account combines hypotheses1–3: behavioral weighting changes what information is worth preserving, different heads require different ranks, and a representation needs a coordinate-reading rule as well as a retained write subspace. Hypotheses4–6 are the highest-priority unresolved mechanistic alternatives: local metric transfer, finite nonlinearity, and metric estimation noise.

H502 should distinguish4 from5 before increasing model capacity. Compute local output-Fisher errors on the actual frozen evaluation pairs and compare them with finite signed interventions at prescribed scales0.1/1/2 for all nine heads and all three fixed maps. If the local ordering already fails, study receiver metric generalization or source covariance assumptions; if local and small-scale ordering agree but unit/double-scale ordering changes, finite nonlinearity becomes the better target. If both agree but uncertainty is large, inspect concentration and metric estimation stability. These are decision rules, not pre-committed conclusions. H502 will be diagnostic on reused pairs; a new confirmation panel should follow a specific discriminating claim.

The broad research goal remains open. This branch establishes useful data- and objective-dependent approximations; it does not yet establish unique or universal fundamental computational units.
