## H631 — Frozen operator contrast transfers to fresh countries, but remains construction-dependent

The previous turn was progress: H630 separated additive accumulation from interaction and identified bilinear-context sharing as sufficient for the studied sign reversal. H631 prospectively freezes the vector, four original capital reference contexts and all replacement rules. New countries are Portugal, Brazil, Austria, Mexico, Poland, Turkey, Greece and Sweden, absent from the prior eight-country panel. Cross them with "People in … speak", "Residents of … speak", and "The main language spoken in … is". Retain all24 new prompts, eight original “speak” anchors, and four capital references:36 prompts,108 native/±0.02 forwards.

Expected conventional-language targets and a13-language candidate set are fixed before inference. They are task labels, not exhaustive claims about multilingual populations. No prompts are selected on model output, no new subset search occurs, and no direction or operator is fitted to the new panel. “Fresh” means absent from this experiment series' current panel, not absent from model training or every earlier project artifact.

**The precise “People in … speak” contrast transfers to all eight fresh countries.** The native addition-signed intended-token log-probability derivative is negative in all eight (mean−0.274). Sharing only bilinear operating contexts in MLP6–9 makes all eight positive (mean+0.363). Sharing only RMS Jacobians leaves all eight negative (mean−0.256). Full normalized-MLP sharing also makes all eight positive (mean+0.207). The original eight anchors reproduce the same pattern.

**The phenomenon is construction-dependent.** On “Residents of … speak”, seven of eight native derivatives are already positive; bilinear-context sharing raises all eight positive. On the main-language statements, all eight native derivatives are positive; context-sharing leaves seven positive and changes the mean only slightly. Thus fresh identities confirm the narrow mechanism on its original construction, while new wording limits any universal sign claim.

**Competence changes the interpretation.** Both fresh “speak” constructions have only25% correct native top1 and candidate choice. “People in … speak” often predicts English; “Residents of … speak” often predicts “out”, a plausible beginning of a different continuation. The main-language statement has100% correct top1 and candidate choice for the same eight country targets. This contrasts knowledge availability with prompt-dependent continuation preference. The sign pattern transfers even on the two correct “People in” cases, but the full eight-case result cannot be described as preserving or improving eight successfully executed tasks.

A derivative concerns how probability changes locally; a positive derivative need not make the target most probable. No finite-amplitude replacement or generated answer correction was tested here. The shared operators are counterfactual local transformations and need not be realizable as a native prompt. Four-reference averages remain part of the intervention definition.

**A simple English-versus-other-language switch is insufficient.** An offline audit of the frozen results examines token log-probability derivatives. On fresh “People in … speak”, native mean derivatives are−0.274 for expected targets,−0.113 for English, and−0.213 averaged over the other declared languages. English is not simply gaining probability while expected languages lose it. Under context sharing, English rises+0.495, expected targets+0.363, and the remaining-language mean+0.062. This shows heterogeneous lexical effects and continuation competition, not an identity-neutral scalar gain for every language. The token audit is descriptive and did not alter prompts, rules or reported competence.

The result strengthens a construction-conditioned operator account: a fixed direction encounters different bilinear operating contexts, and replacing those contexts can reverse its effect on specified language tokens in a reproducible construction. It weakens the concern that the original sign contrast only memorized the eight old countries. It does not establish an abstract language-answer unit, task-independent operator semantics, or a universal behavior-improvement intervention. Wording sensitivity and incomplete task execution remain central limitations.

Next keep this prospective panel and fixed reference operators, but vary the injected direction using predeclared orthogonal and random controls plus directions from the existing learned span. Test whether the bilinear-context contrast specifically acts on the discovered direction or describes a broader family of responses. Report full-vocabulary geometry and intended-token effects separately; retain the competence-failing cells and avoid tuning directions on their outcomes. This addresses whether the candidate computational unit is a direction, a subspace, or a more general context-conditioned operator.
