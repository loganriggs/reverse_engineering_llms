Source-only uses log source norm. Size adds receiver norm, relative source size and total input-change energy. Geometry adds signed angle and missing-subspace quantities. Geometry+readout adds entropy, confidence, baseline RMS and the predicted change's raw/Fisher energies. Some predictors are algebraically or statistically correlated; ridge coefficients are not causal attributions.

The size-only model reaches.568 log-error R². Adding missing-subspace geometry gives.544, so this chosen geometric model does not improve absolute-error point prediction. Adding readout-related features reaches.754 and Spearman.877. It predicts log effects even better (.894 R², Spearman.945). Thus much of the apparent predictability of absolute error concerns effect magnitude, not which interventions are unusually hard relative to their size. Relative-error prediction is weaker: source-only.007, size.026, geometry.081, full features.270 R². No nested improvement has a confirmatory confidence interval here.

The top10% of cases ranked by the full absolute-error predictor contain55.75% of total error but69.03% of total effect. They are large events, not automatically poor-fidelity events. Conversely, the full relative-error predictor's top10% contain only1.22% of total error and.42% of total effect. This highlights a denominator issue: high error/effect ratios can identify small effects with little contribution to pooled error. These quantities must remain separate when choosing future experiments or defining the canonical approximation's objective.

On the new panel, rank128 leaves an average51.15% of degree-two input-change energy outside its empirical input space (10th/median/90th percentiles37.58/49.88/64.97%). Yet raw output-response recovery was~93%. The native output operator suppresses or combines much of that missing input-feature energy; covering a fraction of input energy is not the same as explaining that fraction of computation. Uncovered fraction correlates weakly with relative difficulty (Spearman.263), while absolute uncovered energy correlates strongly with absolute error (.768), substantially confounded by perturbation size. These associations do not establish the missing input span as the dominant causal error source.

The predicted change's baseline-Fisher energy strongly tracks actual effect magnitude (Spearman.946), but this says little about its direction in probability space. A model can predict that a change will matter without predicting exactly which probabilities move. Next distinguish amplitude calibration from directional miss: compute the optimal scalar rescaling of the predicted write change under the exact baseline Fisher metric using native responses as an explicitly oracle diagnostic; evaluate the finite KL after that rescaling. Also compare raw-energy norm matching. Large improvement would motivate learning a scalar gain; little improvement would motivate richer response directions. The analytic Fisher optimum is not a certified optimum of finite KL and must not be described as one. No oracle result should be presented as a deployable canonical model.
